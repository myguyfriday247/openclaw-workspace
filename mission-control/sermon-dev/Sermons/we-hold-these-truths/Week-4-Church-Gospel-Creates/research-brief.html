Week 4: The Church Is the People the Gospel Creates

Series: We Hold These Truths | Week: 4 of 4

Key Passage: Acts 2:41-47

INTRODUCTION & BURDEN

After establishing that the Bible is God's truth (Week 1), that Jesus is the only way (Week 2), and that the gospel is our only hope for transformation (Week 3), we arrive at the natural conclusion: the church is the family the gospel creates.

Many people today view the church as optional—a religious club you can join or leave. Others see it as a building or a business. But Scripture reveals something radically different: the church is the family that God creates when the gospel takes hold of people's hearts.

The burden: To show that the church isn't something we create or choose—it's what God creates when the gospel transforms us. The church isn't a nice addition to the Christian life; it's the inevitable result of the gospel taking root in people's lives.

OBSERVATION 1: THE CHURCH MATTERS BECAUSE IT IS THE RESULT OF THE GOSPEL (Acts 2:41)

1.1 The Logical Flow: Bible → Jesus → Gospel → Church

This series has built a logical foundation:

  • Week 1: The Bible is God's source of truth—we can trust it
  • Week 2: Jesus is the only way—we need Him, not religion
  • Week 3: The Gospel is the power—we can't change ourselves
  • Week 4: The Church is the family—gospel transformation creates community

This isn't arbitrary progression. The logic flows naturally:

  • Because the Bible is true, we can trust its message about Jesus
  • Because Jesus is who He claims to be, His death and resurrection is good news
  • Because the gospel is the power of God, it produces something
  • Because the gospel produces change, it creates a people—a family

1.2 The Gospel Creates a Family, Not Just Believers

When the gospel takes root, it doesn't just produce individual believers—it creates a community. This is central to Derwin Grey and Transformation Church's teaching.

🏈 Derwin Grey & Transformation Church

Dr. Derwin L. Gray is the founding and lead pastor of Transformation Church in Indian Land, South Carolina—a multiethnic, multigenerational, mission-shaped community. His book "Building a Multiethnic Church: A Gospel Vision of Love, Grace, and Reconciliation in a Divided World" emphasizes that the gospel creates a family that transcends ethnic and cultural boundaries.

Key insight from Derwin Grey:

"Jesus broke down every dividing wall so we could be a part of a new multiethnic family. This is intrinsic to the gospel. Our differences reflect God."
Derwin Grey

From his teaching on racial reconciliation:

"The good news is that the Bible has a lot to say about how to heal our persistent racial divides... how God from the beginning envisioned a reconciled multiethnic family in loving community, reflecting His beauty and healing presence in the world."
Derwin Grey, "How to Heal Our Racial Divide"

1.3 This Is God's Intent From the Beginning

Derwin Grey emphasizes that the vision of a multiethnic family isn't an afterthought—it's God's original intent:

"God from the beginning envisioned a reconciled multiethnic family in loving community, reflecting His beauty and healing presence in the world." — Derwin Grey

This connects to the gospel story:

  • Genesis 1-2: God creates humanity in His image, male and female, from one blood (Acts 17:26)
  • Genesis 12: God calls Abraham to bless all nations—not to create one ethnic group
  • Acts 2: At Pentecost, the gospel creates a family from every nation
  • Revelation 7: The final vision is "a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages"

1.4 The Church Is the Evidence of Gospel Power

If the gospel is truly the power of God (Romans 1:16), it will produce something visible:

"And the Lord added to their number daily those who were being saved." — Acts 2:47

The church isn't a human organization that happens to use religious language. It's the supernatural result of the gospel transforming lives. When the gospel takes hold:

  • People are forgiven and reconciled to God
  • People are reconciled to each other
  • A new community is formed
  • Lives are transformed visibly
"The gospel is not just a message about heaven—it's a message that creates a people on earth." — Tim Keller
Tim Keller

SUPPLEMENTARY: WHAT THE CHURCH LOOKS LIKE (FROM ACTS 2)

2.1 The Marks of a Gospel-Created Church

"So those who received his word were baptized, and there were added that day about three thousand souls. And they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers. And awe came upon every soul, and many wonders and signs were being done through the apostles. And all who believed were together and had all things in common. And they were selling their possessions and goods and distributing them to anyone, as anyone had need. And day by day, attending the temple together and breaking bread in their homes, they received their food with glad and generous hearts, praising God and having favor with all the people. And the Lord added to their number daily those who were being saved." — Acts 2:41-47

1. Devoted to Teaching (v. 42)

The early church prioritized learning God's Word. This wasn't casual—"devoted" implies commitment and consistency.

2. Devoted to Fellowship (v. 42)

Koinonia—deep community, sharing life together. Not just attending a service, but genuine relationships.

3. Devoted to Breaking Bread (v. 42, 46)

Communion (the Lord's Supper) and meals together. The gospel creates intimacy.

4. Devoted to Prayer (v. 42)

Dependent on God together. Community prayer.

5. Shared Resources (v. 44-45)

They held things in common. No one said anything was their own. They sold possessions to meet needs.

6. Praise and Joy (v. 46-47a)

"With glad and generous hearts... praising God." The gospel produces joy, not drudgery.

7. Favor with People (v. 47a)

The community was attractive. The gospel produces something beautiful.

8. Gospel Growth (v. 47b)

"The Lord added to their number daily." The church grows because God adds people.

2.2 This Is Church, Not a Club

What the Church Is NOT

  • A building you go to
  • A religious organization
  • A club you join
  • A social network
  • A consumer product

What the Church IS

  • The family God creates
  • A new covenant community
  • The body of Christ
  • The temple of the Holy Spirit
  • A people called out for God's purposes

Observation 1: The Church Matters Because It Is the Result of the Gospel (Acts 2:41)

"So those who received his word were baptized, and there were added that day about three thousand souls." — Acts 2:41

TRUTH/OBSERVATION

The church isn't something we create or choose—it's what God creates when the gospel transforms us. The moment the gospel takes root, a family is formed. These three thousand weren't starting a club; they were being added to something God was building.

APPLICATION

If you call yourself a Christian but treat the church as optional, you contradict the very nature of what God has done. The gospel doesn't just save individuals—it creates a family. You cannot have the benefits of the gospel without being part of the family the gospel creates.

PRACTICAL NEXT STEP QUESTIONS

  • Have I genuinely been added to God's family, or am I trying to follow Jesus alone?
  • Do I see my relationship with other believers as family, or just religious attendance?
  • What would change in my life if I truly believed the gospel created me to be part of a family?

Observation 2: The Church Matters Because It Is Where We Grow Spiritually (Acts 2:42)

"And they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers." — Acts 2:42

TRUTH/OBSERVATION

Growth doesn't happen in isolation. The early church devoted themselves to four practices: teaching (God's Word), fellowship (community), breaking bread (Communion and meals together), and prayer (dependence on God). These aren't optional activities—they're how we grow.

APPLICATION

You cannot grow spiritually on your own. The church isn't a place where Christians happen to meet—it's the environment where God designed us to mature. If you're not consistently engaged in teaching, fellowship, communion, and prayer with other believers, you're trying to grow without the very means God ordained.

PRACTICAL NEXT STEP QUESTIONS

  • Am I consistently devoted to learning God's Word with other believers?
  • Do I have genuine fellowship (deep relationships), or just superficial connections?
  • When was the last time I participated in Communion and prayer with the church?
  • What would it look like to commit to all four practices this season?

Observation 3: The Church Matters Because It Is Where the Gospel Shapes Our Lives (Acts 2:43-46)

"And all who believed were together and had all things in common. And they were selling their possessions and goods and distributing them to anyone, as anyone had need. And day by day, attending the temple together and breaking bread in their homes, they received their food with glad and generous hearts, praising God..." — Acts 2:44-46

TRUTH/OBSERVATION

The gospel doesn't just save us—it shapes how we live. The early church was generous (selling possessions to meet needs), unified (holding all things in common), and sacrificial (meeting needs regardless of cost). The gospel produces visible transformation, not just internal beliefs.

APPLICATION

True gospel transformation is visible. If your life looks no different from the world—if you're not generous, not unified with other believers, not willing to sacrifice for others—you may have the gospel's explanation but not its power. The church is where the gospel shapes real lives with real change.

PRACTICAL NEXT STEP QUESTIONS

  • Is my life visibly shaped by the gospel, or just doctrinally informed?
  • Am I generous with my resources, or do I hold tightly to what I have?
  • Am I unified with other believers, or do I isolate myself?
  • What would sacrificial love look like in my context this week?

Observation 4: The Church Matters Because It Displays and Advances God's Mission (Acts 2:47)

"...praising God and having favor with all the people. And the Lord added to their number daily those who were being saved." — Acts 2:47

TRUTH/OBSERVATION

The church isn't just for believers—it's God's instrument to display and advance His mission. The early church attracted others and God added to their number daily. When the church lives as God designed, it becomes a lighthouse that draws people to Christ. We exist to make God known.

APPLICATION

The church's mission is bigger than your spiritual growth. You're not just saved to go to heaven—you're saved to be part of God's mission in the world. The question isn't just "How does the church help me?" but "How does my life in the church advance God's mission to the world?"

PRACTICAL NEXT STEP QUESTIONS

  • Does my life in the church make Christ attractive to others?
  • Am I actively participating in God's mission, or just consuming church benefits?
  • Who can I invite to experience the family that the gospel creates?
  • What would it look like to be part of adding to God's family this month?

OBJECTION HANDLING

"I've been hurt by the church before."

Response: So have many others. But the church isn't a human organization—it's what God creates through the gospel. The failures of human churches don't negate God's design. The answer to broken churches isn't no church—it's a better understanding of what the church is supposed to be.

"I can follow Jesus without church."

Response: Actually, no. The gospel creates a family—you can't have the benefits of the family without being in the family. Jesus didn't die just to give you a ticket to heaven; He died to create a people. The Christian life isn't meant to be lived alone.

"Church is just a building."

Response: That's exactly backwards. The church is the people—the family God creates. Buildings are just meeting places. What matters is the community, the fellowship, the family bonds that the gospel creates.

"I don't need community, I'm an introvert."

Response: The issue isn't whether you want community—it's whether the gospel has created you into a family. Being an introvert doesn't exempt you from being part of God's family. The gospel creates community regardless of our personality preferences.
