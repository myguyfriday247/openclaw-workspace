INTRODUCTION & BURDEN

In a culture that celebrates pluralism and insists that "all paths lead to God," the claim that Jesus is the only way is considered offensive, narrow, and intolerant. Yet this is precisely what Jesus said: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me" (John 14:6).

This sermon is prophetic—counter-cultural in the best sense. Most churches have avoided this topic in recent years due to fear of offense. But avoidance has not made the culture more accepting; it has made the church less distinctive.

The burden: To present the exclusive claims of Jesus with both boldness and compassion, helping people understand why this is not hate speech but good news—and why the "all paths" alternative actually diminishes all religions.

SECTION 1: THE REAL QUESTION — WHY DO WE WANT TO GET TO GOD?

1.1 The Universal Longing

Every human being has an innate desire to know God. This isn't cultural—it's baked into creation. Pascal famously said, "There is a God-shaped vacuum in the heart of each person which only God can fill."

"People are certainly made for God—and they will not rest, nor can they rest, until they rest in Him." — Augustine
Augustine

1.2 Why People Seek God (Survey Data)

  • Purpose — "Why am I here? What's my purpose?"
  • Meaning — "Is there something more than this?"
  • Peace — "I need peace in my life"
  • Direction — "I need to know the right path"
  • Connection — "I want to feel less alone"

1.3 The Deeper Question

Before asking "how do we get to God?" we need to ask "why do we want to get to God?"

  • Is it fear of judgment?
  • Curiosity about the unknown?
  • Desire for meaning?
  • A genuine relationship?

The answer matters because it determines what kind of "god" we're looking for. If we just want comfort, we'll create a comfortable god. If we want truth, we'll seek the true God.

⚠️ The Danger of False Answers

When people seek God on their own terms, they often find:

  • An idol of their own making
  • A god who approves of their lifestyle
  • A "higher power" vague enough to mean anything

Problem: These "gods" cannot deliver what the true God can.

SECTION 2: THE REAL PROBLEM — WHAT IS KEEPING US FROM GOD?

2.1 The Barrier of Sin

"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." — Romans 3:23

Sin isn't just "breaking rules"—it's rebellion against a holy God. It's saying to God, "I don't want you ruling over me." This creates a barrier that no human effort can overcome.

"But your iniquities have made a separation between you and your God, and your sins have hidden his face from you so that he does not hear." — Isaiah 59:2

2.2 The Problem Is Deeper Than Behavior

  • We're not just sinners because we sin—we sin because we're sinners.
  • Our problem isn't primarily behavioral (what we do) but relational (who we are)
  • We don't need improvement—we need transformation
  • We don't need a better version of ourselves—we need a new nature

2.3 The Threefold Problem

Problem Description Result
Guilt We are condemned Shame, fear, hiding
Powerlessness We cannot save ourselves Frustration, despair
Alienation We are separated from God Emptiness, void

2.4 The Illustration: The Bridge

Imagine a canyon with humanity on one side and God on the other. The canyon is too wide to jump, too deep to swim, and too steep to climb. Sin is the canyon. Every religious attempt to "build a bridge" (be good enough, follow the rules, meditate, etc.) fails because the problem isn't lack of effort—it's the gap itself.

The only solution: Someone from the other side must build the bridge and come to us.

SECTION 3: THE REAL REQUIREMENT — WHAT WOULD IT TAKE TO GET BACK TO GOD?

3.1 What God Demands

God is holy. He cannot simply overlook sin. Justice requires payment. But God is also love—and He doesn't want to leave us in our separation.

The tension: How can a holy God forgive sin without compromising His holiness?

3.2 The Cost of Reconciliation

"For the wages of sin is death..." — Romans 6:23a

Death is the payment required. Something must die. Either we pay the price (eternal separation from God), or something/Someone pays on our behalf.

3.3 What It Would Take

  • Perfect righteousness — Someone who never sinned
  • Substitutionary death — Someone who takes our place
  • Divine power — Someone who can actually save
  • Personal access — Someone who can bring us to God

Only One Person Meets These Requirements

No religious leader:

  • Claimed to be God (Jesus did)
  • Died for the sins of others (Jesus did)
  • Rose from the dead (Jesus did)
  • Promised to return (Jesus did)

SECTION 4: THE REAL ANSWER — HOW JESUS BRINGS US BACK TO GOD

4.1 The Key Passage: 2 Corinthians 5:18-21

"All this is from God, who through Christ reconciled us to himself and gave us the ministry of reconciliation; that is, in Christ God was reconciling the world to himself, not counting their trespasses against them, and he has committed to us the message of reconciliation. Therefore, we are ambassadors for Christ, God making his appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God. For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God." — 2 Corinthians 5:18-21

4.2 The Five "Alls" of Reconciliation

Phrase Meaning
All this is from God Salvation is wholly God's initiative
All have sinned Every person needs reconciliation
All things Christ renews all of creation
All who believe Salvation is available to all who trust
Always Our reconciliation is eternal

4.3 What Jesus Did

Act Scripture Result
Incarnation John 1:14 God came to us
Life of obedience Heb 4:15 Perfect righteousness
Death 1 Pet 2:24 Paid for our sin
Resurrection Rom 4:25 Proved it worked
Ascension Eph 4:8 Prepares our place

4.4 The Exchange

"For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God." — 2 Corinthians 5:21

The greatest exchange in history:

  • He took our sin
  • He gave us His righteousness
  • He bore our curse
  • He gave us His blessing
  • He died our death
  • He gave us His life
"God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God." — 2 Corinthians 5:21

4.5 Why Exclusivity is Actually Good News

The Paradox of Exclusive Truth

People often say: "Isn't it arrogant to claim Jesus is the only way?"

Response: Actually, it's the most inclusive claim ever made. Here's why:

  • All can come — No prerequisites, no credentials needed
  • All are invited — Not just the religious or moral
  • All are equal — No hierarchy of worthiness
  • All can be saved — Not just those in the right culture

Exclusive? Yes. But it's an exclusive offer, not an exclusive club.

SECTION 5: THE REAL RESPONSE — WHAT WILL YOU DO WITH THIS?

5.1 The Urgency

Paul writes: "We implore you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God." This isn't optional—it's urgent. The word "implore" (Greek: parakaleo) conveys passionate pleading.

The urgency isn't because God is impatient—it's because life is short and eternity is forever.

5.2 The Response Required

  • Recognize the problem (your sin)
  • Repent (turn from your way to His)
  • Receive (trust in what Jesus did)
  • Rest (stop trying to earn it)

5.3 Not a Religion—A Relationship

Christianity isn't about rules—it's about reconciliation. It's not about behavior modification—it's about being made new. It's not about going to church—it's about being God's children.

"But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God." — John 1:12

5.4 The Invitation is Open

Today, Right Now

If you've never trusted in Jesus, you can do that right now. Not "become religious" or "try harder"—trust in what He has already done.

A simple prayer:

"God, I know I need you. I admit I've lived my own way and have fallen short. Thank you for sending Jesus to die for my sin and rise again. I trust in what He did for me. I receive Your gift of reconciliation. Amen."

SECTION 7: ILLUSTRATIONS

7.1 The Bridge Illustration

Two cliffs. Humanity on one side, God on the other. Sin is the chasm—too wide to jump, too deep to swim. Every religion tries to build a bridge with good works, rules, or meditation. But bridges built from our side always collapse. Jesus didn't build a bridge—He BECAME the bridge.

7.2 The Hostage Situation

Imagine being held captive by a captor you cannot defeat. Then someone comes—willing to pay your debt, take your place, and set you free. That's what Jesus did. He's not a tyrant demanding entrance—He's a Savior who paid the price so you could be free.

7.3 The Life Vest Illustration

You're drowning in a lake. Swimming hard but going under. A lifeguard doesn't hand you a book on swimming—he jumps in and pulls you out. That's what Jesus does. He doesn't give us rules to save ourselves—He saves us by His grace.

7.4 The Foreign Currency Illustration

Imagine trying to buy something with the wrong currency. You work so hard trying to make your money work—but it doesn't. Then someone offers to exchange their currency for yours, at great cost to themselves. That's grace—you can't earn it; you can only receive it.

7.5 Ravi Zacharias Story

Ravi Zacharias was a suicidal teenager in India who had tried every religion and philosophy. Nothing worked. He picked up a Bible, read John 14:6 ("I am the way, the truth, and the life"), and realized Jesus wasn't claiming to be a way—He was claiming to be the way. It was this exclusivity that gave him certainty. He went on to become one of the most influential apologists of our time.

SECTION 8: OBJECTION HANDLING

"Isn't it arrogant to claim Jesus is the only way?"

Response: It's actually the most humble claim. We're not saying we've figured it out—we're saying Jesus} claimed it, not us. And His resurrection validated it. Plus, it's actually more inclusive: anyone can come, regardless of background.

"What about good people of other faiths? Won't they be saved?"

Response: This assumes "good people" is the standard. But the Bible says "all have sinned." The question isn't how good we are—it's whether we trust in what Jesus did. Many sincere people in other religions are trusting in false hopes. Our concern isn't about them—it's about presenting the truth in love.

"What about those who never heard of Jesus?"

Response: That's exactly why we share the gospel! But we also trust that God is just and will deal with people righteously. Our job isn't to judge who gets in—it's to share the Way.

"This is offensive. Why can't you just let people believe what they want?"

Response: I do let people believe what they want. But I also believe what I believe is true—and I care enough about you to share it. If I found out your house was on fire, I wouldn't stay quiet to avoid being "offensive."

"What about the 'straight gate' in Matthew 7? Weren't most people excluded?"

Response: The narrow gate is narrow because it requires faith alone, not moral effort. It's "narrow" because you can't carry your stuff through it—you have to come empty-handed. That's actually good news!

CLOSING CHALLENGE

The Reconciliation Invitation

Paul writes with passion: "We implore you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God."

This is the heart of the gospel. Not rules to follow, but a relationship to receive. Not a religion to join, but reconciliation to accept.

Three Responses

  1. If you've never trusted in Jesus — Do it today. Trust in what He did, not what you do.
  2. If you've been trying to earn God's favor — Stop. Receive what is already offered.
  3. If you're already a Christian — Live as an ambassador. Share this message.

The question is not: "Will there be other ways?"

The question is: "Will you take this way?"
