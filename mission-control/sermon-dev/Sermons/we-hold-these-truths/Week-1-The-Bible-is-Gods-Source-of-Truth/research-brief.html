Series: We Hold These Truths | Week: 1 of 4

Key Passage: 2 Timothy 3:14-17

INTRODUCTION & BURDEN

We live in a "post-truth" era where absolute truth is increasingly rejected. People are told to "follow their heart," "find their truth," or "create their own reality." The result is a generation experiencing unprecedented instability—mental health crises, fractured relationships, and a loss of purpose.

This series begins with the most foundational question: Where do we find truth? Before we can know what we believe, we must know that we can know. The Bible is not one option among many—it is God's reliable, authoritative source of truth for every area of life.

The burden: To establish the Bible as trustworthy and authoritative so that people will build their lives on the solid rock of God's Word rather than the shifting sand of cultural opinion.

SECTION 1: THE CULTURAL MOMENT

1.1 The "Follow Your Heart" Problem

We are living in what has been called the "post-truth" era. Moral relativism has become the default worldview for many—particularly younger generations. The phrases "your truth" and "my truth" have replaced "truth."

The irony: Those who reject absolute truth still act as if it exists. They still expect courts to be just, doctors to tell the truth, and bridges to hold. Deep down, humans know we need something solid to stand on.

1.2 The Instability of Feelings-Based Truth

When people build their lives on feelings, culture, or personal experience, they're building on shifting sand. These foundations change with:

  • Emotional states (hormones, exhaustion, stress)
  • Cultural trends (what was acceptable 10 years ago isn't now)
  • Personal experiences (your experience differs from mine)
  • Peer pressure (truth becomes whatever the group says)
"The heart of man is not simply evil, but it is also empty—it will fill itself with whatever is available." — Tim Keller
Tim Keller

1.3 The Warning: Psalm 11:3

"If the foundations be destroyed, what can the righteous do?" — Psalm 11:3

This is the question facing our generation. When the foundation of absolute truth is removed, everything built on it collapses. But there is a firm foundation—God's Word.

SECTION 3: THE BIBLE IS GOD'S WORD

3.1 What "God-Breathed" Means

"All Scripture is breathed out by God..." — 2 Timothy 3:16

The Greek word is theopneustos—"breathed out by God." This is not casual "inspiration" like a creative idea. It means Scripture is God's voice.

Just as God breathed into Adam the breath of life (Genesis 2:7), God breathes into Scripture His life-giving truth. The Bible is not merely human words about God—it is God's words to us.

3.2 The Doctrine of Verbal Plenary Inspiration

The Bible teaches that God inspired every word:

  • Verbal: Every word is inspired, not just the ideas
  • Plenary: All parts are inspired—law, history, poetry, prophecy, gospels, epistles
"For no prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit." — 2 Peter 1:21

3.3 Jesus' View of Scripture

If we trust Jesus, we must trust His view of the Bible:

  • Matthew 5:18: "Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law"
  • Jesus quoted Deuteronomy against Satan in Matthew 4 (every word matters)
  • Jesus treated the Old Testament as factual history
  • Jesus promised the Holy Spirit would bring all His words to mind (John 14:26)
"We can no more disassociate the Jesus of the New Testament from the God of the Old Testament than we can separate the stream from the spring." — John Stott
John Stott

3.4 Authority vs. Advice

The Critical Distinction

Advice can be ignored—you consider it and choose whether to follow it.

Authority demands response—it's not optional, it's binding.

The Bible is not advice from God. It is the authoritative voice of God.

"For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit, of joints and of marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart." — Hebrews 4:12

SECTION 4: THE BIBLE IS SUFFICIENT FOR LIFE

4.1 The Four Uses of Scripture (2 Timothy 3:16-17)

"All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work." — 2 Timothy 3:16-17

1. Teaching (Didaskalia)

Shows us truth about God, ourselves, and reality. Answers life's deepest questions. The Bible doesn't just inform—it transforms.

2. Rebuking (Elegmos)

Exposes sin, false beliefs, and wrong paths. The Law is a mirror that shows our true condition. Without Scripture, we can't see ourselves clearly.

3. Correcting (Epanorthosis)

Shows the path back to right relationship. Provides instruction for restoration. Not just "don't do this" but "do this instead."

4. Training in Righteousness (Paideia)

Equips for daily Christian living. "Trains" implies progressive formation—not just information but transformation. The goal is Christlikeness.

4.2 "Every Good Work"

God's Word prepares us for service—not just for heaven later, but for life now. The Bible is not a escape from reality; it's the tool for living effectively in reality.

"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path." — Psalm 119:105

4.3 The Result: Completeness

"That the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work." The Word of God makes us complete—not partially equipped, but fully prepared.

SECTION 6: ILLUSTRATIONS

6.1 The Building Analogy

Jesus said two builders—one built on sand (feelings, culture, personal experience), the other on rock (God's Word). When the storms came, only the rock foundation held. Your life will face storms. What are you building on?

6.2 The Map Analogy

Without truth, you're lost but don't know it. The Bible is like a GPS that knows the territory perfectly. The question isn't whether the map is accurate—it's whether you'll follow it.

6.3 The Surgeon Analogy

Would you trust a surgeon who said, "I'm just giving my opinion—take it or leave it"? Of course not. You want the truth. The Bible isn't opinion—it's the diagnostic of eternity.

6.4 The Contact Lens Analogy

Before contact lenses, you thought you knew what reality looked like—but you were blurry. The Bible is like putting on contacts—you suddenly see reality clearly for the first time.

6.5 The Dead Sea Scrolls Story

In 1947, a shepherd boy threw a stone into a cave and heard pottery shatter. He found ancient scrolls that were over 1,000 years older than any known biblical manuscripts. When scholars compared them, they matched with 99%+ accuracy. This proved the Bible had been transmitted faithfully for millennia.

OBJECTION HANDLING

"The Bible was written by men, so it can't be God's Word."

Response: Yes, it was written by men—but men who were "carried along by the Holy Spirit" (2 Peter 1:21). The笛子 of authorship is both human and divine. Your DNA has a human author (your parents) but a divine author (God's design). Similarly, Scripture is fully human and fully divine.

"The Bible has contradictions."

Response: Apparent contradictions usually stem from misunderstanding context, genre, or translation. The Bible's 66 books written over 1,500 years show remarkable unity. Scholars have found no irreconcilable contradictions when properly understood.

"The Bible is outdated—it doesn't apply to modern life."

Response: The core truths about God, humanity, sin, and salvation never change. The Bible addresses timeless human struggles. Its principles for life are as relevant today as when written.

"I've tried reading the Bible and didn't get anything out of it."

Response: The issue may be approach, not content. The Bible is God's Word—it expects us to come with humility and prayer. Try starting with the gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke, John) and ask God to speak.
