## Big Idea
God reveals himself as **Yahweh-Shalom** in the middle of fear, guilt, oppression, and unfinished conflict. Peace in Judges 6 is not first the removal of trouble, but the presence, word, and covenant mercy of God.

---

## Theological Burden
Judges 6 teaches that biblical peace is deeper than emotional calm. Israel is impoverished, compromised, hiding, and under judgment. Gideon is fearful and hesitant. Yet God says, **“Peace be to you. Do not fear; you shall not die”** (Judg. 6:23). The burden of the sermon is this: **God is our peace because he reconciles fearful sinners to himself and steadies them before circumstances change.**

---

## Passage Overview (Judges 6:1–40)

### 1. Israel’s Sin and Midianite Oppression (6:1–6)
Israel again does evil, and the Lord gives them into Midian’s hand for seven years. Midian devastates harvests, livestock, and ordinary life. Israel hides in caves and dens.

**Key emphasis:** their loss of peace is covenantal before it is political.

### 2. The Prophet’s Rebuke (6:7–10)
When Israel cries out, God first sends a prophet. Before rescue comes explanation. The problem is not merely Midianite strength, but Israel’s disobedience.

**Key emphasis:** peace requires truth about sin.

### 3. Gideon’s Call (6:11–18)
Gideon is threshing wheat in a winepress, hiding from Midian. The angel of the Lord addresses him as a “mighty man of valor” and promises divine presence.

**Key emphasis:** God addresses Gideon by calling before Gideon reflects courage.

### 4. Divine Assurance and Yahweh-Shalom (6:19–24)
Gideon offers food; fire consumes it; Gideon fears death. The Lord replies, **“Peace be to you. Do not fear; you shall not die.”** Gideon builds an altar and names it **Yahweh-Shalom**.

**Key emphasis:** this is the theological center of the chapter.

### 5. Baal Must Fall (6:25–32)
God commands Gideon to tear down his father’s altar to Baal and cut down the Asherah. Peace is immediately linked to repentance and reform.

**Key emphasis:** the God of peace is not tolerant of rival worship.

### 6. Spirit, Mobilization, and the Fleece (6:33–40)
The Spirit clothes Gideon, armies gather, and Gideon asks for confirming signs. God patiently stoops to his weakness.

**Key emphasis:** peace does not erase trembling overnight.

## Word Studies

### Shalom (שָׁלוֹם)
**Meaning range:** peace, wholeness, soundness, welfare, safety, well-being, completeness.

In Scripture, *shalom* is not merely the absence of conflict. It includes:
- right relationship with God
- covenant well-being
- inner and communal wholeness
- order under God’s rule

In Judges 6, peace is spoken **before** Midian is defeated. So peace here is:
- objective before subjective
- relational before emotional
- theological before therapeutic

### Yahweh-Shalom (יְהוָה שָׁלוֹם)
Occurs in Judges 6:24.

The name means:
- the Lord is peace
- the Lord is the source and ground of peace
- peace is found in God himself, not in human strength or improved conditions

The altar is memorial theology: Gideon marks the place where terror was answered by divine mercy.

## Historical / Cultural Context

### Midianite Raiding
Midian and her allies function as mobile raiders, likely striking during harvest season. Their goal is not only occupation but economic depletion.

This creates:
- chronic insecurity
- food scarcity
- humiliation
- loss of normal rhythms of life

### Hiding in Caves
Israel, meant to dwell securely in the promised land, now lives as fugitives. This is covenant shame made visible.

### Winepress Imagery
Threshing wheat normally happened in open air where wind could carry off chaff. A winepress is the wrong place. Gideon is working in concealment.

**Preaching payoff:** God announces peace in a place of fear-shaped adaptation.

### Household Idolatry
Baal and Asherah are not abstract national problems; they are in Gideon’s own family system. Before God deals with Midian outside, he deals with Baal inside.

## Major Exegetical Insights

1. **The chapter begins with Israel’s evil, not Midian’s power.**  
   The narrator interprets the crisis spiritually.

2. **God sends a prophet before a deliverer.**  
   Repentance and interpretation precede rescue.

3. **Gideon is called while afraid.**  
   God’s calling does not wait for human confidence.

4. **“The LORD is with you” is central.**  
   Divine presence is the ground of peace and courage.

5. **Judges 6:23–24 is the hinge of the chapter.**  
   Yahweh-Shalom comes from God’s word of life to one who expected death.

6. **The altar is built before the battle is won.**  
   Peace is confessed in unresolved circumstances.

7. **Baal’s altar must be torn down.**  
   Peace is moral and covenantal, not merely emotional.

8. **The fleece shows patient condescension.**  
   God works with weak faith without endorsing unbelief.

## Theological Themes

### 1. Peace After Guilt, Not Apart from It
The prophet’s sermon shows Israel’s suffering is tied to covenant failure. Peace does not begin with self-soothing but with God confronting rebellion.

### 2. Peace in the Presence of Holiness
Gideon fears death because he has encountered the living God. The deepest problem is not anxiety but alienation.

### 3. Peace Before Circumstances Change
Midian still exists when Gideon names the altar. This matters pastorally: God’s peace can be real before the crisis is over.

### 4. Peace That Purifies
The God of peace immediately orders idol demolition. Biblical peace restores right worship.

### 5. Peace and Weakness
Gideon is not a natural hero. God’s peace comes to weak people and then steadies them for obedience.

---

## Christological Trajectory

Judges 6 should lead naturally to Christ.

- Gideon fears death in God’s presence; in Christ we have **peace with God** (Rom. 5:1).
- Gideon hears, “Peace be to you”; Jesus says, **“Peace I leave with you”** (John 14:27).
- Gideon builds an altar called Yahweh-Shalom; Paul says, **“He himself is our peace”** (Eph. 2:14).
- Gideon’s peace is anticipatory; Christ’s peace is accomplished through the cross.

**Strong Christ line:**  
What Gideon knew by altar-name, the church knows by incarnation, atonement, and resurrection: **the Lord is our peace because Christ himself is our peace.**

## Objections and Pastoral Tensions

### Objection 1: “If God is our peace, why is Gideon still afraid?”
Because biblical peace is not the same thing as instant emotional composure. Gideon still trembles, questions, and asks for signs. Peace begins in God’s word and presence, then grows in experience.

### Objection 2: “Does this text promise that God will always change our circumstances quickly?”
No. Judges 6 shows peace announced before deliverance is complete. God gives himself first.

### Objection 3: “Is peace here just inner calm?”
No. *Shalom* includes wholeness, covenant order, safety, well-being, and restored relationship.

### Objection 4: “Why does the God of peace command confrontation?”
Because biblical peace is not passive tolerance of evil. It is the restoration of right order under God’s holy reign.

## Sermon Application

### For the anxious
God’s peace is not built on your emotional steadiness. Gideon is shaky, and God still speaks peace.

### For the ashamed
This chapter begins with Israel’s guilt, yet the Lord still comes near. Peace is mercy for rebels.

### For the exhausted
Midianite oppression resembles chronic depletion. This text speaks to people worn down by repeated loss.

### For the hiding
God meets Gideon in concealment. Hiddenness does not disqualify someone from divine calling.

### For the idol-tolerant
You cannot protect Baal and enjoy Yahweh-Shalom. Peace requires reordered worship.

### For the church
Congregations often want comfort without repentance. Judges 6 refuses that split.

## Final Pastoral Synthesis
Judges 6 reveals Yahweh-Shalom in a chapter full of fear, compromise, poverty, and hesitation. That is exactly the point. God does not reveal himself as peace to people who already feel strong. He reveals himself as peace to needy people who are hiding, compromised, and unable to save themselves. He speaks peace, tears down idols, and begins deliverance. In Christ, this peace is no longer only an altar-name; it is embodied, accomplished, and offered to the church.
