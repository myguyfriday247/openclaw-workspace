# Week 4 Research Brief — God Is Our Peace

**Series:** God Is... Names of God  
**Title:** God Is Our Peace  
**Passage:** Judges 6  
**Date:** June 7, 2026  

## Big Idea
God reveals himself as **Yahweh-Shalom** not by removing every threat immediately, but by meeting fearful, compromised, afflicted people with his presence, his word, and his saving purpose. In Judges 6, peace is not first a feeling; it is God’s covenant assurance in the middle of fear, guilt, and conflict.

## Main Theological Burden
This sermon should show that **peace in Scripture is deeper than calmness**. Israel is oppressed, guilty, hiding, and spiritually compromised. Gideon is fearful and hesitant. Yet God speaks: **“Peace be to you. Do not fear; you shall not die”** (Judg. 6:23). The altar name **Yahweh-Shalom** means the Lord himself is the source of wholeness, safety, reconciliation, and rest. Peace begins with God’s presence and word before circumstances are resolved.

---

## 1) Passage Overview and Flow

### A. Israel’s Sin and Midianite Oppression (6:1–6)
- Israel again “did what was evil in the sight of the LORD.”
- God gives them into Midian’s hand for seven years.
- Midian, Amalek, and the peoples of the east devastate crops and livestock.
- Israel hides in dens and caves.

**Preaching angle:** their lack of peace is not random; it is covenantally connected to rebellion.

### B. God Sends a Prophet Before a Deliverer (6:7–10)
- Israel cries out.
- God first sends a prophet, not a military solution.
- The prophet reminds them of redemption from Egypt and confronts their disobedience.

**Preaching angle:** God interprets suffering before removing it. Peace requires truth.

### C. Gideon’s Call in Fear (6:11–18)
- Gideon threshes wheat in a winepress to hide from Midian.
- The angel of the LORD appears and calls him “mighty man of valor.”
- Gideon questions God’s presence and the absence of wonders.

**Preaching angle:** God calls fearful people before they feel ready.

### D. Divine Revelation and Peace (6:19–24)
- Gideon prepares an offering.
- Fire consumes it.
- Gideon fears death after seeing the angel of the LORD.
- God says, “Peace be to you. Do not fear; you shall not die.”
- Gideon builds an altar and names it **Yahweh-Shalom**.

**This is the theological center of the chapter.**

### E. Peace Requires the Tearing Down of Idols (6:25–32)
- Gideon must tear down his father’s altar to Baal and cut down the Asherah.
- He obeys, though fearfully, by night.
- He is renamed Jerubbaal.

**Preaching angle:** God’s peace is holy. It does not coexist peacefully with idols.

### F. Gideon’s Weak Faith and the Fleece (6:33–40)
- War gathers.
- The Spirit clothes Gideon.
- Gideon still asks for signs.

**Preaching angle:** peace does not mean instant maturity. God is patient with weak faith.

## 2) Key Word Studies

## Shalom (שָׁלוֹם)
**Meaning range:** peace, wholeness, completeness, welfare, safety, well-being, soundness, flourishing.

This is crucial: in the Bible, **shalom is not merely the absence of conflict**. It includes:
- right relationship with God
- covenant well-being
- personal and communal wholeness
- safety and stability
- ordered life under God’s blessing

In Judges 6, shalom is spoken to a man who is still in danger externally. So peace here is not first circumstantial ease, but divine assurance and reconciliation.

## Yahweh-Shalom (יְהוָה שָׁלוֹם)
Occurs in Judges 6:24.

This altar name means:
- **The LORD is peace**
- the LORD is the source and guarantor of peace
- peace is grounded in God’s character, not Israel’s strength

The altar is a memorial: Gideon marks the place where terror was answered by divine word.

## 3) Historical and Cultural Context

### Midianite Raiding Culture
The Midianites in Judges 6 appear as mobile raiders, not merely settled imperial occupiers. They invade at harvest, consume produce, devastate the economy, and leave Israel impoverished.

This creates:
- chronic insecurity
- survival fear
- agricultural collapse
- humiliation in the land God gave them

### Hiding in Dens and Caves
This imagery shows more than military weakness. Israel’s covenant identity is collapsing. The people meant to dwell securely in God’s land are now living like fugitives.

### Winepress Instead of Threshing Floor
Threshing wheat in a winepress is abnormal. Gideon is adapting normal work to fearful conditions.

**Homiletical payoff:** peace is announced in hiddenness, not triumph.

### Baal and Asherah in the Household
The family altar to Baal reveals the deeper issue: Israel’s problem is not only Midian outside, but idolatry inside. Peace is not simply deliverance from enemies; it requires repentance.

## 4) Major Exegetical Insights

1. **The chapter begins with Israel’s evil, not Midian’s power.**  
   Scripture interprets trouble theologically.

2. **God answers the cry with a prophet before a warrior.**  
   Repentance must precede restoration.

3. **Gideon’s fear is central to the narrative.**  
   He is not introduced as bold, but hiding.

4. **“The LORD is with you” (6:12) is the key promise.**  
   Divine presence is the foundation of peace.

5. **Verse 23 is the hinge of the chapter.**  
   “Peace be to you. Do not fear; you shall not die.”

6. **The altar is built before the battle is won.**  
   Gideon confesses peace while circumstances remain dangerous.

7. **Peace is followed by idol demolition.**  
   The God of peace wages war against false gods.

8. **The fleece shows God’s patience with weak faith.**  
   Peace and trembling can coexist in a believer’s experience.

---

## 5) Theological Themes

## A. Peace and Reconciliation
Gideon fears death because he has encountered the holy God. God’s word of peace is fundamentally a word of **mercy and preservation**.

## B. Peace in the Midst of Conflict
This passage does not say, “God is our peace because there are no enemies.” It says God is peace **while Midian still exists**.

## C. Peace and Holiness
God’s peace is not sentimental niceness. It requires Baal’s altar to come down.

## D. Peace and Divine Presence
The repeated promise is not “you are enough,” but “I will be with you” (6:16).

## E. Peace and Weakness
God does not wait until Gideon becomes heroic. He reveals peace to him in weakness.

---

## 6) Christological Trajectory

Judges 6 should ultimately move toward Christ.

- Gideon fears death in God’s presence; in Christ we receive **peace with God** (Rom. 5:1).
- Gideon hears a divine word of peace; Jesus says, **“Peace I leave with you”** (John 14:27).
- Yahweh-Shalom finds fuller revelation in **Ephesians 2:14: “He himself is our peace.”**
- Christ does not merely announce peace; he accomplishes it through the cross.

**Christ-centered line:**  
What Gideon knew by altar-name, the church knows by incarnation and cross: **the Lord is our peace because Christ himself is our peace.**

## 8) Objections and Pastoral Tensions

## Objection 1: “If God is our peace, why is Gideon still afraid?”
Because biblical peace is not identical to emotional steadiness. Gideon remains hesitant, yet God is still his peace. Peace begins in God’s word and presence before it is fully felt in experience.

## Objection 2: “Does God’s peace mean life gets easier?”
Not necessarily. Judges 6 shows that peace can be spoken before deliverance is complete. God often gives his people himself before he changes their situation.

## Objection 3: “Is peace here just inner calm?”
No. Shalom includes:
- reconciliation with God
- covenant order
- moral restoration
- communal well-being
- eventual deliverance

## Objection 4: “Why would the God of peace call for tearing down altars and going to battle?”
Because biblical peace is not passive tolerance of evil. God restores peace by confronting what destroys his people.

## 9) Sermon Application

## For the anxious
God’s peace is not built on your emotional control. Gideon is shaky, but God still speaks peace.

## For the ashamed
This chapter begins with guilt and compromise, yet God still comes near. Peace is mercy for sinners.

## For the exhausted
Israel is worn down by repeated loss. This text speaks to people living under chronic depletion.

## For those hiding
Gideon is found in concealment. God meets people in reduced, fearful places.

## For idol-tolerant believers
You cannot cling to Baal and ask for Yahweh’s peace. Peace includes repentance.

## For the church
Congregations may want comfort without reformation. Judges 6 says peace comes with truth and cleansing.

- Don’t separate peace from repentance and holiness.

## Final Pastoral Synthesis
Judges 6 introduces **Yahweh-Shalom** in a chapter full of fear, poverty, idolatry, and hesitation. That is the point. God does not reveal himself as peace to people who already have themselves together. He reveals himself as peace to needy people who expect death and cannot save themselves. He speaks peace, demands repentance, and begins deliverance. In Christ, this peace is no longer only an altar-name; it is embodied, purchased, and proclaimed.
