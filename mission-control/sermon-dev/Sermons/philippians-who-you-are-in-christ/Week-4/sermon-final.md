# SERMON MANUSCRIPT — WEEK 4
## "Knowing Christ"
**Series:** Who You Are in Christ
**Text:** Philippians 3:4b-14
**Date:** August 23, 2026

---

## OPENER

I want to start with a question.

What's on your résumé?

I don't mean your actual résumé — I mean the thing you carry around inside you that tells you who you are. The thing that makes you think, "I'm okay. I've got something to offer. I've done some things right."

Maybe it's your performance. You've been a good parent, a good employee, a good friend. You've shown up. You've done the right things.

Maybe it's your heritage. You grew up in church. You know the Bible. You can talk about theology.

Maybe it's your moral track record. You've got a clean record. You're not like *those* people.

Whatever it is — I want to tell you something. And I want you to sit with it.

Paul had more résumé than almost anyone in the first century. He was circumcised on the eighth day. He was a Benjaminite — from the tribe of Benjamin, the same tribe as King David. He was a Hebrew of Hebrews. A Pharisite. As for righteousness under the law — blameless.

In other words: he had everything. Every credential. Every right answer. Every box checked.

And then he met Jesus.

And what did he do with that résumé? He said: "Whatever were gains to me I now consider loss for the sake of Christ. What is more, I consider everything a loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord."

He called his résumé *garbage*.

Now — that word in Greek is *skubalon*. It's the word for human waste. feces. Paul was not being subtle. He was saying: everything I thought made me valuable — it's worthless. Compared to knowing Christ, it's actual trash.

That's a hard word. Because all of us have a résumé. And the question this message is asking is: **What are you still holding onto that God is asking you to let go of?**

---

## POINT 1: True righteousness is found in Christ, not in yourself

Paul dismantles every credential he once trusted. Circumcision, heritage, legal standing — he lists them, then calls them garbage.

Not "pretty good." Not "helpful but not necessary." *Garbage.*

And here's why he does it. He's not trying to be shocking. He's trying to be clear: **your best efforts will never outscore grace.**

Think about it this way. A participation trophy at the Olympics. Imagine walking up to the medal ceremony and pulling out your "everyone's a winner" ribbon. It doesn't matter how hard you tried. The medal table isn't for people who showed up. It's for people who were found in Christ.

I think a lot of us are still trying to earn what was already given. We think: if I just pray enough, read my Bible enough, serve enough, give enough — then God will accept me. Then I'll be enough.

But Paul says: you've already been given the righteousness that comes through faith. It's not earned. It's received. And once you understand that — really understand it — your résumé stops being your identity. Christ is your identity. And Christ is enough.

**Application:** The question is not "Am I doing enough?" The question is "Am I in Christ?" Because if you're in Christ, you are already accepted. Not because of what you did. Because of what He did.

---

## POINT 2: Knowing Christ is the prize — not just having salvation

Here's where Paul gets radical. Most people want the gift. Paul wanted the Giver.

He writes: "I want to know Christ — yes, to know the power of his resurrection and participation in his sufferings."

Not "I want to be saved from hell." Not "I want to go to heaven." He wants *the power of resurrection*. He wants to *suffer like Christ*. He wants *participation*.

This is the difference between people who merely avoid hell and people who are radically transformed. One group has a ticket. The other group has a relationship. And those are not the same thing.

**Illustration:** A student who memorizes the answers to pass the test versus one who studies to actually understand the subject. One is trying to get by. The other is trying to *know* it. God didn't save you to pass. He saved you to *know*. To know Him. To walk with Him. To let Him shape you from the inside out.

I meet a lot of people who want to go to heaven but don't want to know God. Who want the destination without the journey. Who want the cure without the Physician.

Paul says: not me. Not my life. I want to know the One who saved me. I want to know Him more. I want to know Him at the cost of everything else.

---

## POINT 3: Let go of what you thought made you valuable

Paul doesn't just say he gave up his credentials. He says he *counted them as loss*. There's a deliberate mental posture here — he's actively re-evaluating. Not because those things were evil. Because they became *obstacles* between him and Christ.

Here's the thing nobody talks about: **you can have good things that are actually blocking a better thing.**

I think about someone carrying a briefcase full of gold coins across a desert. That briefcase is heavy. And then someone offers them water — the water they need to survive — but they can't accept it because their hands are full. They can't let go of the gold. And they're dying of thirst.

That's what Paul did. He looked at his briefcase — all his credentials, all his righteousness — and then he looked at Christ, and he poured the coins out in the sand. Because he realized: *this is blocking me from the only thing that actually matters.*

**Application:** What's in your briefcase? What's the thing you keep holding onto that God is asking you to set down? It might not be a bad thing. It might just be a *smaller* thing that He's asking you to release so you can grab hold of something bigger.

---

## POINT 4: Forget what lies behind — this is not a rearview mirror faith

Paul says: "Forgetting what is behind."

Now — that doesn't mean pretend your past didn't happen. It doesn't mean deny your story. It means: *stop letting your past be your identity.*

Past victories can be just as dangerous as past failures. Why? Because victories can make you think you're already there. That you've arrived. That the race is won.

I think about a runner who keeps looking back at the starting line. Every time they do, they stumble. Because looking back costs you ground. And the race is forward.

**Here's the question: what are you looking back at?**

Maybe it's a failure. Something you did, something you didn't do. And you've been carrying that for years — the shame, the regret, the "what if."

Maybe it's a victory. A season when God showed up in a powerful way. And you've been living in that memory — trying to recapture it, recreate it, when God wants to take you somewhere new.

Either way — the call is forward. Not because the past doesn't matter. Because the future is calling. And you can't run a race while looking in the rearview mirror.

---

## POINT 5: Press on with holy aggression — Christ is the goal

Here's the turn that changes everything.

Paul says: "I press on to take hold of that for which Christ Jesus took hold of me."

Notice — *Christ* took hold of Paul *first*. Grace comes before the pursuit. God reaches for you before you reach for Him. The initiative is always His.

But — and this is important — that reality doesn't make Paul passive. It ignites him. He *strains forward*. He *presses on*. He runs hard.

Not to earn the prize. To *lay hold* of what was already offered.

**Illustration:** A father promises his son a bike. The son doesn't earn it. It's given. But the son still runs to the garage every morning to check on it. To make sure it's still there. To anticipate it. That's what grace does. It doesn't make you lazy. It makes you *hungry*.

The Christian life is not a slow walk to a finish line you hope to cross. It's a sprint. It's a holy aggression — a relentless pursuit of the One who first pursued you. And the reason you run is because you've tasted Him. You've seen Him. And nothing else compares.

---

## CLOSING APPLICATION

Where are you still trusting your résumé?

Maybe it's your performance as a parent. Your clean record. Your church attendance. Your moral track record. Whatever it is — I want to tell you what the Spirit is saying today.

Lay it down. Not because those things are evil. Because they're *small*. They will never make you holy. Only knowing Christ will make you holy.

So stop polishing your briefcase. Stop rehearsing your credentials. Stop carrying the weight of what you thought made you valuable.

Pick up your cross. And follow.

That's the door to the life you were made for.

*"Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal to win the prize for which God has called me heavenward in Christ Jesus."*
