# Week 4: Knowing Christ / Pressing On

**Scripture:** Philippians 3:4b–14

> "But whatever were gains to me I now consider loss for the sake of Christ. What is more, I consider everything a loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord, for whose sake I have lost all things. I consider them garbage, that I may gain Christ and be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own that comes from the law, but that which is through faith in Christ—the righteousness from God on the basis of faith. I want to know Christ—yes, to know the power of his resurrection and participation in his sufferings, becoming like him in his death, and so, somehow, attaining to the resurrection from the dead. Not that I have already obtained all this, or have already arrived at my goal, but I press on to take hold of that for which Christ Jesus took hold of me. Brothers and sisters, I do not consider myself yet to have taken hold of it. But one thing I do: Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal to win the prize for which God has called me heavenward in Christ Jesus."

---

## Main Points

### 1. True Righteousness Is Found in Christ, Not in Yourself (v. 9)

Paul dismantles every credential he once trusted. Circumcision, heritage, legal standing — he lists them and then says *garbage*. That's not a typo; that's intentional shock value. He's saying all my self-earned righteousness is worthless compared to what God gives through faith. The crowd who packed the pews in Jerusalem would have been furious. But Paul isn't trying to be shocking. He's trying to be clear: **your best efforts will never outscore grace.**

*Illustration:* Imagine bringing a participation trophy to the Olympics. That's what self-righteousness looks like when you bring it before a holy God. The medal table isn't for people who showed up. It's for people who were found in Christ.

---

### 2. Knowing Christ Is the Prize — Not Just Having Salvation (v. 10–11)

Most people want the *gift*. Paul wants the *Giver*. He writes "I want to know Christ" — not "I want to be saved from hell" or "I want to go to heaven." He wants the resurrection's *power*. He wants to *suffer like him*. He wants *participation*, not just a ticket. This is the difference between people who merely avoid hell and people who are radically transformed.

*Illustration:* A student who memorizes the answers to pass the test versus one who studies to actually understand the subject. One is just trying to get by. The other is trying to *know* it. God doesn't want you to just pass. He wants you to *know*.

---

### 3. Let Go of What You Thought Made You Valuable (v. 4b–8)

Paul doesn't just say he gave up his credentials. He says he *counted them as loss*. There's a deliberate mental posture here — he's actively re-evaluating everything. Not because those things were evil, but because they became *obstacles* between him and Christ. When you realize who Christ is, your old résumé stops impressing you.

*Illustration:* A man carrying a briefcase full of gold coins across a desert. Someone offers him water. He can't accept the water and keep carrying the coins — he's too burdened. Eventually he has to pour them out. That's what Paul did. He looked at his briefcase, then at Christ, and chose Christ.

---

### 4. Forget What Lies Behind — This Is Not a Rearview Mirror Faith (v. 13)

"Forgetting what is behind" is not pretend it didn't happen. It's refusing to let it be your identity anymore. Paul doesn't say *deny* the past. He says *forget* it — like taking your hand off the steering wheel and looking forward. Past victories can be as dangerous as past failures if they make you think you're already there.

*Illustration:* A runner who keeps looking back at the starting line. Every time they do, they stumble. The race is forward. The prize is ahead. Looking back — at your failures or your fancies — costs you ground you can't afford to lose.

---

### 5. Press On with Holy Aggression — Christ Is the Goal (v. 12–14)

"I press on to take hold of that for which Christ Jesus took hold of me." Notice — Christ took hold of Paul *first*. Grace comes before the pursuit. But that reality doesn't make Paul passive. It ignites him. He strains forward. He runs hard. Not to *earn* the prize but to *lay hold* of what was already offered. This is holy ambition — the kind that drives you toward godliness because you're in love with the one who called you.

*Illustration:* A father promises his son a bike. The son doesn't earn it. It's given. But the son still runs to the garage every morning to check on it. Grace doesn't make you lazy. It makes you *hungry*.

---

## Closing Application

Where are you still trusting your résumé? Maybe it's your performance as a parent. Your clean record. Your church attendance. Your moral track record. The Spirit invites you today to lay it down — not because it's evil, but because it's *small*. It will never make you holy. Only knowing Christ will. So stop polishing your briefcase. Pick up your cross. That's the door to the life you were made for.
