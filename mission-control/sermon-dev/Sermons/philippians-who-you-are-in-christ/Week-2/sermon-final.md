# SERMON MANUSCRIPT — WEEK 2
## "The Mind of Christ: Humility That Rewrites Everything"
**Series:** Who You Are in Christ
**Text:** Philippians 2:1-11
**Date:** August 9, 2026

---

## OPENER

I want to start with a question.

When was the last time someone accused you of being too humble?

Never. Right? No one has ever said that. "Hey, you know what your problem is? You're just not humble enough. You need to work on that."

Nobody says that. Because humility — real humility — is countercultural. It goes against every instinct we have. We live in a world that rewards self-promotion. That tells you to put yourself first. That says if you don't advocate for yourself, nobody else will.

And then Paul sits down and writes: "In humility, count others more significant than yourselves."

And then he doesn't leave it as a nice idea. He shows us what it actually looks like. And when you see it — when you really see it — it rewires everything.

---

## POINT 1: It's not just a command — it's an appeal

Paul opens with four words that aren't doubt — they're intensity:

"If there's any encouragement in Christ, any comfort from love, any participation in the Spirit, any affection and sympathy..."

He's not hedging. He's piling on. He's saying: *Because of everything you have in Christ, live like it.*

Here's what I think we get wrong about humility. We think it's a command — "be humble" — like God is just telling us to try harder. Pull yourself up by your bootstraps. Be less proud.

But Paul doesn't start with a command. He starts with a gift. "You have encouragement in Christ. You have comfort from love. You have participation in the Spirit." Before he says "be humble," he says "you already belong."

The Christian life isn't a ladder we climb. It's a table we sit at — already fed, already loved, already accepted. And because of that, we can afford to be gentle with others. We can put others first — not because we're trying to earn something, but because we've already been given everything.

**Illustration:** Think about the last time someone forgave you when you didn't deserve it. Didn't that grace make you want to be gentler, not prouder? That's the logic Paul is working with here. Love received creates love given.

So when you read "be humble" — hear it as: *You've been loved this much. Now live like it.*

---

## POINT 2: Complete this joy — think like Christ

Paul says: "Complete my joy by being like-minded, having the same love, being one in heart and mind."

This is the test. Not "tolerate people." Not "get along." *Be like-minded.* United. Aligned. Not uniform — same personality, same opinions, same style. No. But *one in heart and purpose.*

The opposite of humility isn't arrogance. It's self-occupation. It's being so consumed with your own agenda, your own reputation, your own comfort, that you have no room left for anyone else.

I think about this in traffic. Nobody in their right mind thinks, "You know what, I'm going to be humble in traffic today." But that's exactly where it shows up, isn't it? Someone cuts you off and your first instinct is: *That person is an idiot.* Not "that person might be having the worst day of their life." Just — idiot.

That's self-occupation. And humility starts when you realize the world doesn't revolve around you — and neither do its problems.

**Application:** Ask yourself this week: *What would it look like to prioritize someone else's peace over my preference?* Not because they deserve it. Because Christ gave me what I didn't deserve first.

---

## POINT 3: The mind of Christ — emptied, not empty

This is the center of the passage. And it's where things get radical.

Paul describes Christ's humility in five stages:

**First:** He did nothing from selfish ambition. No personal agenda driving Him. Everything He did came from the Father, not from Himself.

**Second:** He counted others more significant than Himself. Not because they were more talented, or more deserving — but because they mattered. Period.

**Third:** He looked to the interests of others. Every decision filtered through love. Not "what's best for me?" but "what's best for them?"

**Fourth:** He emptied Himself. Now — and this is important — He didn't empty Himself of deity. He didn't stop being God. But He added humanity. He became serve-able. He took on the form of a servant. Not fake servanthood. Not appearance. Reality.

**Fifth:** He became obedient to the point of death — even death on a cross.

Now pause there. That's the lowest point in the universe. The God who made everything, dying on a cross, naked, mocked, abandoned. That should have been the end. That should have been humiliation.

But here's the logic that rewrites everything: **the cross wasn't just suffering. It was service.**

Jesus didn't lose when they killed Him. He was doing what He came to do. He was serving us. Even in the moment of maximum rejection, He was giving Himself away.

**Illustration:** Picture a cup filled with water. Now picture someone pouring that water out to give it to someone else who's thirsty. The cup doesn't become worthless when it's poured out. It becomes useful. That's what Jesus did. He poured Himself out — not loss of self, but giving of self. That's what makes it so radical. It's not an example we try harder to follow. It's a pattern we lean into by faith.

---

## POINT 4: The name above every name

Here's the turn nobody sees coming.

"Humble yourself" isn't the end of the story. Because Jesus didn't stay low. God highly exalted Him. Gave Him the name above every name. Every knee will bow.

But listen — the path to that exaltation was humility. The path up was down. The path forward was backward. The burial became the breakthrough.

**If you're in a season of feeling unseen, overlooked, underestimated — Paul says that's the exact pathway Jesus walked.**

Don't despise humble places. They're launchpads.

**Illustration:** Think about a seed buried in dirt. It looks like it's done. Nobody sees it. It's dark down there. But burial is where growth starts. The cross looked like defeat. It was the most celebrated moment in the history of the universe.

If God has you in a low place right now — a job that's beneath your skills, a season where nobody notices you, a ministry opportunity that feels small — don't write it off. That might be exactly where He's preparing your launchpad.

---

## POINT 5: Work out your salvation — the power of internal humility

Paul closes this section with a line that trips people up: "Work out your salvation with fear and trembling."

Wait — doesn't that contradict grace? If we need to work it out, aren't we earning it?

No. Paul says *work out* what God *works in*. The humility of Christ doesn't just save you — it shapes you. It's not just the door to heaven. It's the pattern for living.

And "fear and trembling" doesn't mean terror. It means holy reverence. It means taking seriously that God is actively at work inside you. You don't manufacture humility on your own. You surrender to the One who already embodied it — and He reshapes you from the inside.

**Application:** This isn't "try harder." It's "trust deeper." It's saying: *God, I can't be humble on my own. But I can let You make me humble. Work in me. Change me. Give me eyes to see others the way You see them.*

---

## CLOSING APPLICATION

Philippians 2 answers one question better than almost any passage in the Bible:

*What does a transformed person look like?*

Answer: **Someone who stops demanding their own way and starts seeking the good of others — not because they have to, but because they've seen what Jesus did for them.**

The pastor's kids fighting over the remote. The coworker who takes credit for your work. The family member who never says thank you. The social media feed that makes you envious. Every one of those moments is an invitation to practice the mind of Christ.

**This week, identify one relationship where you're prone to selfish ambition or conceit. Then ask God to give you one specific opportunity — just one — to count that person more significant than yourself.**

Not a perfect person. Not a dramatic sacrifice. Just one step of humility.

That's how the mind of Christ works in real life — not in theory, but in Tuesday.
