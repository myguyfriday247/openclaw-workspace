Week 1: God Says I Am His Child - Outline

Series: Defined - Who God Says You Are Week: Week 1 Date: April 12, 2026 Central Passage: Romans 8:1-17 Core Truth: You are not an orphan trying to earn a home — you are a child who’s already home. Stop performing for identity. Start living from it.

🎣 INTRODUCTION (HOOK)

“How many of you grew up thinking Christianity was about trying harder? Trying to be good enough. Trying to pray enough. Trying to read your Bible enough. Trying to not mess up enough. If you raised your hand, you’re not alone — and this series is for you.

Because here’s what God wants you to know this week: You’re not an orphan trying to earn a home. You’re a child who’s already home.”

Series Context: This is Week 1 of “Defined” — a 4-week series on who God says you are.

📋 MAIN POINTS

POINT 1: Adoption, Not Just Forgiveness

Truth: God doesn’t just save us FROM sin — He brings us INTO His family.

Exposition: - Forgiveness deals with guilt. Adoption deals with identity. - When you forgive someone, you clear a debt. When you adopt someone, you give them your name, your inheritance, your family. - God didn’t just clear our sin debt — He made us His children. - Romans 8:14-17: “All who are led by the Spirit are sons of God… You received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, ‘Abba, Father.’” - The word “Abba” is intimate — it’s “Daddy.” That’s the relationship God wants.

Illustration: “The Self-Made Myth” - We never say “self-made child” because being a child isn’t earned. You’re chosen, not produced. - A baby doesn’t earn being born into a family — they receive it. - Same with God: you didn’t earn adoption, you received it.

Application: - If God just forgave you, you’d still be outside the family looking in. - But He adopted you. You’re in. You’re His. - That changes everything: you have an inheritance, a Father, a future.

Action Steps (3): 1. This week, pray using the word “Father” — notice the intimacy 2. When you fail, say “I’m still His child” out loud — fight the orphan voice 3. Thank God for your adoption in a prayer this week

POINT 2: Chosen Before We Were Worthy

Truth: God chose us before we could do anything to earn it — before creation.

Exposition: - Ephesians 1:4-5: “He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world… predestined us to adoption.” - God didn’t choose you because He foresaw you’d be good enough. He chose you because He wanted to. - Before you were born, before you could accept or reject Him — He said yes. - That’s not because of your worth — it’s because of His love.

Illustration: “The Unconditional Offer” - Imagine someone gives you $1 million — no strings attached. Just: here’s a million, it’s yours. - Then they say, “But wait — you have to keep earning it. Every day, prove you deserve it.” - Wait, what? I thought it was a gift? - That’s what we do with God. He gives us everything, and we keep trying to earn it.

Application: - You can’t earn what was already given. - Stop trying to earn what you already possess. - The gift is already yours — receive it.

Action Steps (3): 1. Read Ephesians 1:3-14 this week — highlight every spiritual blessing you have 2. When tempted to earn God’s love, remember: the adoption is already done 3. Thank God that your adoption doesn’t depend on your performance

POINT 3: The Spiritual Orphan Mindset

Truth: Many Christians live as spiritual orphans — performing for acceptance, afraid of failure, basing worth on performance.

Exposition: - An orphan always feels like they have to prove themselves. - A child knows they belong. - The orphan thinks: “If I don’t perform, I won’t be loved.” - The child thinks: “I’m loved. I belong. I’m in.” - Most Christians live like orphans. They base their worth on: Did I pray enough? Read enough? Serve enough? Witness enough? - Their self-worth is contingent on performance. And they never feel like they’ve done enough.

Illustration: Psychology Research — “Contingent Self-Esteem” - Psychologists call this “contingent self-worth” — when your sense of value depends on meeting conditions. - Research shows: people with high contingent self-esteem experience more anxiety, more shame, more fear of failure. - Their identity is always on the line. - That’s how many Christians live. That’s the orphan mindset.

Application: - The orphan always feels like they haven’t done enough. - The child knows they already belong. - Which one are you living like?

Action Steps (3): 1. Notice this week: when do you feel like you have to prove yourself to God? 2. Write down 3 truths about who you are in Christ (from Romans 8) 3. When you mess up this week, fight the lie: “I’m still His child”

POINT 5: Living From Our Identity

Truth: Once you know whose you are, you know who you are. Stop performing — start being.

Exposition: - You don’t try to become a child of God. You already are one. - Now join what your Dad is doing. - It’s not about adding God to your life. It’s about joining His work. - When you know you’re an heir, you can stop performing. You can rest. You can serve from overflow, not obligation.

Illustration: LeBron James - LeBron — arguably the greatest player ever — is 40 now. Numbers are down. Not leading in scoring. - But the Lakers are winning. And LeBron? He’s thriving. - He’s not trying to prove he’s still the best scorer. He’s playing a different role — defender, connector, “glue guy.” - Why? Because he already knows who he is. - He doesn’t need to score to prove he’s great. He’s already the greatest. His identity isn’t on trial. - So he can play differently. Sacrifice. Thrive in a role that’s not about him.

Application: - Are you trying to earn through performance what you already possess through identity? - LeBron knows he’s great — so he doesn’t have to prove it every game. - You know you’re God’s child — so you don’t have to earn His love every day. - Stop scoring to prove your worth. Join what your Dad is doing.

Action Steps (3): 1. This week, ask: “What is God doing that I get to be part of?” (not “What can I do for God?”) 2. Choose one thing to stop trying to earn this week 3. Join what God is doing — find one opportunity to serve from overflow, not obligation

🔚 CONCLUSION

“There’s a story about a family that adopted a child. This child had been through a lot — abandonment, foster homes, nothing stable. The family brought them in, gave them a room, dressed them in nice clothes, fed them warm meals.

But every night, the parents would look outside and see their child sleeping on the porch.

They’d say, ‘Come inside! This is your home!’

And the child would say, ‘I can’t. I haven’t earned it yet. I haven’t done enough to deserve a bed inside.’

Here’s the thing: God looks at us the same way. He’s saying, ‘Come inside! I’ve already given you everything! Why are you still sleeping on the porch?’

You didn’t do anything to get this. You can’t do anything to lose it. You don’t have to keep striving — you can BE.

You are God’s child. Not because of what you’ve done. Because of what Christ has done.”

🎯 BOTTOM LINE

“Stop performing for identity. Start living from it. You are God’s beloved child — not because of what you’ve done, but because of what Christ has done.”
