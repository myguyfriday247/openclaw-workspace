Week 1: God Says I Am His Child

Series: Defined - Who God Says You Are | Date: April 12, 2026

Key Passages: John 1:12, Ephesians 1:4-5, 2 Corinthians 5:17

INTRODUCTION & BURDEN

At the core of the gospel is not just forgiveness—it's adoption. God doesn't just save us from sin; He brings us into His family. This means your identity is no longer defined by your past, your performance, or others' opinions, but by the Father who chose you and calls you His own.

Many people live as spiritual orphans—constantly striving for approval, afraid of failure, and unsure of their worth. But in Christ, we are fully known and fully loved. This message invites us to stop performing for identity and start living from it, resting in the truth that we are sons and daughters of God.

The burden: To help people understand that their identity is received, not achieved—and that this changes everything about how they live.

SECTION 1: THEOLOGICAL FOUNDATION

1.1 Adoption in the Biblical Narrative

Adoption in the ancient world was a significant legal act, but biblical adoption goes far beyond Roman or Greco-Roman customs. In the Old Testament, God repeatedly calls Israel His son (Exodus 4:22-23, Hosea 11:1). This father-son relationship was foundational to Israel's identity as God's chosen people.

In the New Testament, adoption becomes available to all who trust in Christ. The Greek word used is "huiothesia" (υἱοθεσία), which literally means "son-placement" or "adoption as sons." Paul uses this word deliberately to emphasize not just our position but our privilege.

Key Theological Points:

  • Adoption is not earned—it's a gift (Ephesians 1:5)
  • Adoption is relational—not merely legal
  • Adoption secures inheritance (Romans 8:17)
  • Adoption gives us the Spirit of sonship (Romans 8:15)

1.2 The Nature of Our Adoption

We are chosen before creation. Ephesians 1:4 tells us God "chose us in him before the foundation of the world." This election is not based on our foreseen faith but on God's sovereign grace.

We are adopted, not merely forgiven. Forgiveness deals with guilt; adoption deals with identity. When God forgives us, He treats us as if we never sin. When He adopts us, He treats us as if we were always His children.

We share in Christ's inheritance. Romans 8:17 declares we are "heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ."

1.3 The New Creation Reality

"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come." — 2 Corinthians 5:17

What "in Christ" means: Union with Christ—our identity is united to Him. His righteousness is credited to our account. His identity becomes our identity.

What "new creation" means: Not improvement of the old nature but replacement. Not behavior modification but transformation. The Greek uses the aorist tense, indicating completed action—not gradual improvement but decisive change.

1.4 The Privilege of Being Children

John 1:12: "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God."

The imagery of children: Children bear their father's name, inherit from their father, reflect their father's character, have access to their father's presence, and are eternally secure in their family position.

The security: Nothing can separate us from God's love (Romans 8:38-39). We are sealed with the Holy Spirit (Ephesians 1:13). We are kept by God's power (1 Peter 1:5).

SECTION 2: BIBLICAL CONTEXT

2.1 John 1:12 - The Right to Become Children

Greek Analysis:

  • "Did receive" (elabon) - actively took hold, received into relationship
  • "Believed in his name" - trust that goes beyond intellectual assent
  • "Right" (exousia) - authority, privilege, power
  • "Become" (genōntai) - literally "come into being"

This verse comes after John the Baptist's testimony about Jesus and before the calling of the first disciples. John is establishing who Jesus is and what He offers.

The contrast: The world did not know Him (1:10). His own people did not receive Him (1:11). BUT all who receive Him...

2.2 Ephesians 1:4-5 - Chosen and Predestined

Verse 4: "Even as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him."

Verse 5: "He predestined us for adoption to himself as sons, through Jesus Christ, according to the purpose of his will."

Theological Implications: Our adoption was planned before time. It's based on God's will, not our worth. It flows from God's love.

2.3 2 Corinthians 5:17 - The New Creation

The "Therefore" draws the conclusion from Christ's substitutionary work. The "If Anyone" is wonderfully inclusive—no qualifications, no prerequisites. The aorist tense indicates completed action—decisive transformation, not gradual improvement.

SECTION 3: COMMON STRUGGLES - THE SPIRITUAL ORPHAN

3.1 What is the Spiritual Orphan?

The "spiritual orphan" describes someone who, despite being Christian, lives as if they have no father. They may intellectually know God as Father but functionally live as if they must earn His love.

"The orphan spirit produces people who are always striving, always performing, always trying to earn what they already have been given." — Tim Keller

3.2 Symptoms of Spiritual Orphanhood

  1. Performance-Based Identity: My worth is based on what I do. I define myself by achievements, roles, accomplishments.
  2. Fear of Rejection: I people-please to secure acceptance. I'm afraid of disappointing God.
  3. Spiritual Insecurity: I'm not sure God really loves me. I wonder if my salvation is secure.
  4. Comparison and Envy: I measure myself against others. I'm competitive with other believers.
  5. Lack of Intimacy: I approach God with fear rather than confidence. Prayer feels like reporting.

3.3 The Root Cause

The spiritual orphan believes lies about their identity: "I must earn what I have," "My performance determines my worth," "God's love is conditional," "I am defined by my past."

SECTION 4: TRANSFORMATION FROM WITHIN

4.1 Identity vs. Behavior

Behavior Modification: Focus on what you do. Try harder to stop sinning. Add spiritual disciplines to improve yourself. Result: Guilt and failure.

Identity Transformation: Focus on who you are in Christ. Let who you are dictate what you do. Rest in what Christ has already accomplished. Result: Freedom and power.

4.2 Living From Identity

When we truly understand our adoption:

  • We can face failure differently: Our identity is secure in Christ. Failure doesn't define us.
  • We can receive correction differently: Discipline is from a loving Father, not an angry judge.
  • We can relate to others differently: We don't need to compare or compete.
  • We can face trials differently: Our security doesn't depend on circumstances.

SECTION 5: PASTORAL APPLICATION

5.1 Questions for Reflection

  • Where am I still trying to prove myself instead of receiving God's love?
  • What part of my life am I still hiding from God?
  • What lie about my identity do I need to replace with God's truth?

5.2 Practical Steps

  1. Meditate on your adoption—make it personal
  2. Receive God's love daily—don't earn it
  3. Replace lies with truth—memorize key verses
  4. Live from your identity—let it shape your decisions

5.3 Addressing Common Objections

"But I still feel like a stranger." Response: Feelings follow facts. Even when you don't feel like God's child, you are.

"But I still sin. How can I be a child of God?" Response: Every child of God struggles with remaining sin. Your identity is secure in Christ.

"What if I lose my salvation?" Response: You can't lose what you didn't earn. Adoption is based on God's promise, not your performance.

SECTION 6: ILLUSTRATIONS

6.1 The Adopted Child

A child adopted into a wealthy family doesn't have to earn an inheritance. It's given. They don't have to perform to receive love—they already have it.

6.2 The Legal Reality

In Roman law, adoption was absolute. The adopted person had all the rights of a biological child. They couldn't be disinherited.

6.3 The Royal Family

When someone marries into royalty, they don't earn the title—they receive it. We are welcomed into the family of the King of kings.

6.4 The Prodigal Son (Luke 15)

The father ran to his son, embraced him, and threw a feast. That's what our Father does. The older son worked for his father but never felt like a son—that's the spiritual orphan.

SECTION 10: OBJECTION HANDLING

"I don't feel adopted." Your feelings don't determine your identity—God's Word does. The Spirit testifies with your spirit that you are a child of God (Romans 8:16).

"But I still sin." The fact that you struggle with sin doesn't mean you're not adopted—it means you're still being sanctified.

"I came from a broken family." That's exactly why you need this message. God is the perfect Father.

"What if I lose my salvation?" You can't lose what you didn't earn. The Spirit is the guarantee of your inheritance (Ephesians 1:14).

CONCLUSION

The message of Week 1 is simple but revolutionary: You are God's child. Not because of what you've done, but because of what Christ has done. Not because you earned it, but because He gave it.

This changes everything. When you know whose you are, you know who you are. And when you know who you are, you can stop striving and start living.

The invitation: Stop performing for identity. Start living from it.
