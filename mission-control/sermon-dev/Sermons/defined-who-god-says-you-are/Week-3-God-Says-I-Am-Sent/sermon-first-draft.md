# God Says I Am Sent

**Series:** Defined — Who God Says You Are  |  Week 3  |  April 26, 2026

**Key Passages:** Matthew 5:13-16, John 15:5, James 2:17
**Core Truth:** True faith produces visible action. But the action doesn't come from striving — it comes from staying connected to Christ. Salt that's lost its saltiness is worthless. Light under a basket is hidden. The call is simple: abide, and fruit will follow.

---

Have you ever been in a conversation where someone says something like: "Yeah, I believe in God. I believe Jesus was a good teacher. I try to live a good life."

And you think: *That's not what the Bible says.*

Because the Bible doesn't say: "Believe the right things and you'll be fine." The Bible says: "Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead" (James 2:17).

That's James. And James is not contradicting Paul. Paul says you're saved by faith, not by works. James is saying something different — and something complementary. He's saying: *real faith produces works.* Saving faith is never passive. Saving faith is alive.

And I want to sit with that this morning, because I think a lot of us have a version of Christianity that looks a lot like what James is criticizing. We think: "I believe. I'm a Christian. I'm fine." And we go through our weeks — same patterns, same habits, same lukewarm existence — and nothing changes. We're alive spiritually, but it doesn't show.

James has a word for that: dead faith. Faith that sits. Faith that watches. Faith that doesn't move.

And the question this message is going to force is a simple one: *Is your faith alive?*

Not "Do you believe?" — you probably do. Not "Are you a Christian?" — I'm asking if there's fruit.

Let's dig in.

Let me pray for us.

*Father, we ask that You would make our faith alive today. Not just belief, but fruit. Not just doctrine, but deed. Show us what it means to live sent — to be so connected to You that Your life flows out of ours. In Jesus' name, amen.*

I want to start with two images Jesus uses in these passages. Salt and light. Two of the most memorable metaphors in all of Scripture.

"You are the salt of the earth... You are the light of the world."

Now, think about what salt does. Salt preserves. It adds flavor. It creates thirst. And in the ancient world, before refrigeration, salt was essential. It kept food from rotting. It made bland food edible. It was valuable — sometimes used as currency.

And then Jesus says: "But if the salt loses its saltiness... it is no longer good for anything except to be thrown out and trampled underfoot."

That's a striking image. Salt that can't salt is worthless. Not even good for gardening. Just garbage.

And then light. "A town built on a hill cannot be hidden. No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bowl. They put it on its stand. And it gives light to everyone in the house."

The point is obvious: you don't hide light. You put it where it can be seen.

So here's the setup: you are salt. You are light. But both only matter if they're doing what salt and light are supposed to do. Salt that doesn't salt — worthless. Light that's hidden — useless.

And I think there's a version of Christianity that looks exactly like that. Christians who have the right label — "I'm a Christian" — but there's no preservation happening. No flavor. No thirst. No light.

That's what dead faith looks like.

But here's where it gets interesting. Jesus doesn't just say "be saltier" or "shine brighter." He doesn't issue a challenge to try harder. What does He say?

In John 15, right after the "I am the vine" metaphor, He says: "Remain in me, as I also remain in you... apart from me you can do nothing."

Nothing. Not "you can do less." Not "you can do small things." You. Can. Do. Nothing.

That's the key. Because if you try to be salt without remaining, you become the most miserable, exhausted version of yourself — white-knuckling through your Christian life, trying to produce fruit that only God can produce, wondering why you're so tired and dry and joyless.

And if you try to shine without remaining — same result. You become a Christian who looks exactly like everyone else, except you go to church sometimes and feel guilty when you don't.

The issue is always connection. Always. Not effort — connection.

Let me say this another way. You were not created to be a light source. You were created to be a *reflector*. There's a difference. A light bulb doesn't generate its own light — it channels electricity. A branch doesn't produce its own fruit — it channels the vine. You are not the origin of the life — you are the conduit.

And when you're connected to the Source, something happens. Fruit comes. Light shines. Salt seasons. Not because you worked really hard. Because you stayed connected.

Here's where James gets so practical. He gives us three specific tests of whether faith is alive:

**Test One: How do you treat people who have nothing to offer you?**

James 2:1-9. He says: "Suppose you see a brother or sister who has no food or clothing, and you say, 'Goodbye, God bless you' — and then you do nothing to help them. Does that faith save them?"

That's convicting. Because how many of us do that? We see someone in need — maybe someone in our church, someone at work, someone on the street — and we say something spiritual and feel spiritual, and we do nothing.

That's not faith. That's performance.

Real faith sees the person. Real faith does something.

**Test Two: How do you treat people who are different from you?**

James 2:8-9. "If you really keep the royal law found in Scripture, 'Love your neighbor as yourself,' you are doing right. But if you show favoritism, you commit sin."

We like people who are like us. Who dress like us. Talk like us. Vote like us. Live in our neighborhoods. Go to our schools.

But James says: that's not faith. That's bias dressed up in religious language.

Real faith extends beyond your tribe.

**Test Three: What comes out of your mouth?**

James 3:1-12. The tongue. The smallest member of the body and the most powerful for destruction. "With the tongue we praise our Lord and Father, and with it we curse human beings."

James says: that shouldn't be. A spring doesn't pour out bitter water. A fig tree doesn't produce olives.

And the implication is staggering: what you say online. What you say about people who disagree with you. What you say about your spouse, your kids, your coworkers, your government.

That's the fruit test. Not "are you a good person?" — "is your speech honoring God?"

---

Now, I want to push past the guilt response, because that's too easy. You can feel guilty after James and stay exactly the same. Guilt is not the goal. Transformation is.

And here's the invitation of this passage: *remain*. Stay connected. Abide.

That word — "remain" — it's the key. Jesus uses it over and over in John 15: "Remain in me... if you remain in me and my words remain in you..."

Remaining is not a passive posture. It's the most active thing you can do. It's the discipline of staying. Of prioritizing the relationship over the performance.

Let me tell you what happens when you remain: your faith stops being dead. It becomes alive. Not perfect. Not painless. Alive.

And alive faith does something.

You know what the world is desperate for? People whose lives don't match the stereotype. People who are kind when it's not convenient. People who forgive when they have every right to hold a grudge. People who are generous when it costs something. People who are honest when a lie would be easier.

That's what salt does. That's what light does. Not because you're trying to be a good Christian — because you're so connected to Christ that His life is showing up in your life.

That's what Jesus is offering you this week. Not a challenge to try harder. An invitation to remain. To stay close. To abide. And then — watch what happens.

Salt without saltiness is worthless. Light under a basket is useless. But a branch that stays connected to the vine? It bears fruit. Automatically. Inevitably. Because that's what connected branches do.

Remain. And watch what your life produces.

2. **Remain before you serve.** Before you add one more thing to your schedule, ask: Am I staying connected to Christ? Am I abiding? Because busy-ness without abiding produces nothing but exhaustion.
