# God Says I Am Sent

**Series:** Defined — Who God Says You Are  |  Week 3  |  April 26, 2026

**Key Passages:** Matthew 5:13-16, John 15:5, James 2:17
**Core Truth:** True faith produces visible action — but that action comes from staying connected to Christ, not from trying harder. Salt that doesn't salt is worthless. Light under a basket is useless. Remain in Him, and fruit follows.

---

Have you ever been in a conversation where someone says: "Yeah, I believe in God. I try to live a good life."

And inside you think: *That's not what the Bible says.*

Because the Bible says: "Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead" (James 2:17).

James isn't contradicting Paul. Paul says you're saved by faith, not by works. James is saying something different — and something complementary. He's saying: *real faith produces works.* Saving faith is never passive. Saving faith is alive.

I want to sit with that this morning, because I think a lot of us have a version of Christianity that looks exactly like what James is criticizing. We say: "I believe. I'm a Christian. I'm fine." And we go through our weeks — same patterns, same habits, nothing changes. Alive spiritually, but it doesn't show.

James calls that dead faith. Faith that sits. Faith that watches. Faith that doesn't move.

The question this message forces: *Is your faith alive?*

Not "Do you believe?" — you probably do. Not "Are you a Christian?" — I'm asking if there's fruit.

Let me pray for us.

*Father, make our faith alive today. Not just belief, but fruit. Not just doctrine, but deed. Show us what it means to live sent. In Jesus' name, amen.*

---

I want to start with two images Jesus uses. Salt and light.

"You are the salt of the earth... You are the light of the world."

Think about what salt does. Salt preserves. It adds flavor. It creates thirst. In the ancient world, before refrigeration, salt was essential — it kept food from rotting. It was so valuable it was sometimes used as currency.

And then Jesus says: "But if the salt loses its saltiness... it is no longer good for anything except to be thrown out and trampled underfoot."

That's striking. Salt that can't salt isn't just ineffective — it's worthless. Not even good for the garden. Just garbage.

And then light. "A town built on a hill cannot be hidden. No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bowl. They put it on its stand, and it gives light to everyone in the house."

You don't hide light. You put it where it can be seen.

Here's the setup: you are salt. You are light. But both only matter if they're doing what salt and light are supposed to do. Salt that doesn't salt — worthless. Light that's hidden — useless.

I think there's a version of Christianity that looks exactly like that. Christians with the right label — "I'm a Christian" — but there's no preservation. No flavor. No thirst. No light.

That's dead faith.

But here's where it gets interesting. Jesus doesn't say "be saltier" or "shine brighter." He says: "Remain in me... apart from me you can do nothing."

Nothing. Not "you can do less." You. Can. Do. Nothing.

That's the key. Because if you try to be salt without remaining, you become the most exhausted version of yourself — white-knuckling through your Christian life, trying to produce fruit that only God can produce, wondering why you're so dry.

And if you try to shine without remaining — same result. You become a Christian who looks exactly like everyone else, except you go to church sometimes and feel guilty when you don't.

The issue is always connection. Not effort — connection.

You were not created to be a light source. You were created to be a *reflector*. A branch, not a root. The channel, not the source.

When you're connected to the Source, something happens. Fruit comes. Light shines. Salt seasons. Not because you worked really hard. Because you stayed connected.

Now here's James getting practical. He gives us three specific tests of whether faith is alive:

**Test One: How do you treat people who have nothing to offer you?**

James 2:1-9. "Suppose you see a brother or sister who has no food or clothing, and you say, 'Goodbye, God bless you' — and then you do nothing to help them. Does that faith save them?"

That's convicting. We see someone in need — maybe someone in our church, someone at work, someone on the street — and we say something spiritual and feel spiritual, and we do nothing.

That's not faith. That's performance.

**Test Two: How do you treat people who are different from you?**

James 2:8-9. "If you really keep the royal law found in Scripture, 'Love your neighbor as yourself,' you are doing right. But if you show favoritism, you commit sin."

We like people like us. But James says: that's not faith. That's bias dressed up in religious language.

**Test Three: What comes out of your mouth?**

James 3:1-12. The tongue. "With the tongue we praise our Lord and Father, and with it we curse human beings."

A spring doesn't pour out bitter water. A fig tree doesn't produce olives.

What you say online. What you say about people who disagree with you. What you say about your spouse, your kids, your coworkers. That's the fruit test.

---

Now, I want to push past the guilt response, because that's too easy. You can feel guilty after James and stay exactly the same. Guilt is not the goal. Transformation is.

The invitation of this passage: *remain*. Stay connected. Abide.

That word — "remain" — it's the key. Jesus uses it over and over: "Remain in me... if you remain in me and my words remain in you..."

Remaining is the most active thing you can do. It's the discipline of staying. Of prioritizing the relationship over the performance.

Here's what happens when you remain: your faith stops being dead. It becomes alive. Not perfect. Not painless. Alive.

And alive faith does something.

The world is desperate for people whose lives don't match the stereotype. People who are kind when it's not convenient. People who forgive when they have every right to hold a grudge. People who are generous when it costs something. People who are honest when a lie would be easier.

That's what salt does. That's what light does. Not because you're trying to be a good Christian — because you're so connected to Christ that His life is showing up in your life.

Salt without saltiness is worthless. Light under a basket is useless. But a branch that stays connected to the vine? It bears fruit. Automatically. Inevitably. Because that's what connected branches do.

Remain. And watch what your life produces.
