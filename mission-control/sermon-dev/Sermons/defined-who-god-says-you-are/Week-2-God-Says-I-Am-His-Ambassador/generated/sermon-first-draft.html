Sermon: God Says I Am His Ambassador

God Says I Am His Ambassador

Series: Defined — Who God Says You Are | Week 2 | April 19, 2026

Key Passages: 2 Corinthians 5:20, 1 Peter 2:9, Philippians 3:20 Core Truth: You are not just saved from something — you are saved to represent Christ wherever you are. Your everyday life is your mission field.

Have you ever noticed that the world has a way of making you feel like your life is smaller than it actually is?

Like — you wake up, you go to work, you come home, you run errands, maybe you scroll your phone for an hour, you go to sleep, and then you do it all again. And somewhere in that loop, you start to wonder: is this all there is? Is there something more meaningful I’m supposed to be doing with my life?

And maybe you’ve asked that question — or maybe you’ve heard someone ask it — and the answer that usually comes back is something like: “I just haven’t found my purpose yet.” Or “I need to figure out what I’m called to do.” Or “Once I find my thing, then I’ll feel like my life matters.”

And I want to name something that’s been living underneath that kind of thinking. Because there’s an assumption buried in it. The assumption is that purpose is something you find — like it’s out there somewhere, hidden, waiting for you to discover it. Like God藏 it and left a map.

But what if purpose isn’t something you find? What if it’s something you receive — right now, in the life you already have?

This is Week 2 of “Defined.” Last week we talked about who you are — God’s child. And if you caught that message, then this week builds directly on it. Because here’s the connection: if you are God’s child, then you represent God’s family. If you belong to Him, you speak for Him. If you’re in His kingdom, you carry His mission.

And that mission isn’t somewhere else. It’s wherever you already are.

Let me pray for us, and then we’ll get into the text.

Father, we thank You that You’ve placed us exactly where we are for a reason. Open our eyes to see our lives as You see them — as mission fields, not waiting rooms. In Jesus’ name, amen.

I want to start with a word that most of us have heard but most of us have never really let sink in. The word is ambassador.

Now, most of us know what an ambassador is — sort of. It’s someone who represents a country in a foreign land. An ambassador from the United States in France, for example, speaks on behalf of the U.S. government. They carry the authority of the President. When they speak, in a sense, the President is speaking through them.

That’s the job description.

But here’s what I don’t think we’ve fully grasped: the word Paul uses here — presbeutes — it shows up only twice in the entire New Testament. Once in Ephesians 6, where Paul says he’s an ambassador in chains. And once right here, in 2 Corinthians 5.

And in both cases, Paul isn’t talking about some official government posting. He’s talking about every single believer.

“We are therefore Christ’s ambassadors, as though God were making His appeal through us” (v. 20).

We. Not a special class of missionaries. Not professional clergy. Not people with special training. We. All of us. Everyone who belongs to Christ.

That means you.

Let that settle for a second. You are an ambassador. Not someday, not when you get more serious, not when you’re more put-together. Right now. Today. You represent Christ wherever you are.

Now, I want to back up and look at the context of what Paul is saying here, because this isn’t a random thought. He’s building an argument. And I don’t want us to miss it.

Look at verses 18 through 20. Paul says: “All this is from God, who through Christ reconciled us to Himself and gave us the ministry of reconciliation — namely, that God was reconciling the world to Himself in Christ, not counting their trespasses against them, and entrusting to us the message of reconciliation.”

So here’s what’s happening: God was reconciling the world to Himself through Christ. That was the work — Christ on the cross, bearing the punishment for sin, making a way for humanity to be restored to God. That work is finished. It’s done.

And then God gave us the ministry of reconciliation. He entrusted the message to us.

Let me say that another way. The most important message in the history of the universe — the message that God is making peace with the world through the death and resurrection of Jesus — that message has been handed to ordinary people. Not to angels. Not to some professional religious class. To us.

And then Paul says the most stunning line: “We are therefore Christ’s ambassadors, as though God were making His appeal through us.”

This is carefully worded. He’s not saying God speaks to us. He’s not saying God speaks about us. He’s saying God speaks through us. When we open our mouths, God is making His appeal. That’s the weight of what an ambassador carries.

And that changes everything about how you live your Monday.

Because if you’re an ambassador, then your job isn’t to figure out your purpose. Your job is to represent the King who’s already placed you where you are.

Let me give you three ways this identity reshapes how you live.

First: Everywhere is your mission field.

There’s a myth that a lot of Christians live with — and it’s this: mission is somewhere else. Mission is for missionaries overseas. Mission is for the people who go. For the rest of us, our everyday life is kind of… secular. You go to work, you come home, you live your normal life, and then maybe on the side you do some spiritual stuff. Pray. Go to church. Read your Bible.

But that is a complete misread of what God is doing.

Look at 1 Peter 2:9. Peter writes: “You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for His own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of Him who called you out of darkness into His marvelous light.”

Peter is talking to regular people. Workers. Slaves. Merchants. Shopkeepers. People scattered across the ancient world. And he says: you are all of those things — chosen, royal, holy, possessed by God — so that you may proclaim His excellencies.

Where? Not somewhere else. Right where you are.

And notice what Peter doesn’t say. He doesn’t say go somewhere else. He says: the reason you have this identity is so that wherever you are, you declare who God is.

You don’t have to go to mission. You’re already in it.

I think one of the most dangerous ideas the enemy has sold the church is that your regular life — your job, your neighborhood, your family — is the waiting room for your real mission. As if the meaningful stuff happens somewhere else, and until then you’re just killing time for God.

No. Your life is the mission. The person you work next to — that’s your mission field. The邻居 across the street — that’s your mission field. Your kids — that’s your mission field. The barista at the coffee shop — that’s your mission field. God didn’t misplace you. He put you exactly where you are.

Second: Your life carries a message before your words do.

Here’s something that I think we underappreciate: people watch before they listen.

I don’t know if you’ve noticed this, but the world is paying attention to Christians. And they are not reading our social media posts or our social media posts or our bumper sticker quotes. They are watching how we live. They are watching how we handle conflict. They are watching how we treat people we don’t agree with. They are watching how we respond when we’re treated unfairly.

And the verdict they render — whether they say it out loud or not — is: Does this person’s life point to something different?

Andy Stanley has this line — I think it’s one of the most convicting in all of Christian leadership literature. He says: “People don’t pick up the New Testament and ask what Christians are about. They look at us.”

That’s both convicting and liberating. It’s convicting because it means our lives actually matter — more than we think. And it’s liberating because it means the gospel doesn’t depend on you being a great speaker. It depends on you being a consistent follower of Jesus. And that’s something every single one of us can be.

But it also means you can’t separate what you believe from how you live. They are linked. Tightly.

The apostle Peter puts it this way in 1 Peter 2:12: “Keep your conduct among the Gentiles honorable, so that when they speak against you as evildoers, they may see your good deeds and glorify God on the day of visitation.”

Peter is saying: they will watch you. And when they see something different in the way you live — something that doesn’t fit their stereotype of Christians — that might just open a door for the gospel.

Your life is a sermon. It’s either one people want to hear more about, or one that confirms every bad thing they already believe. There’s not a third option.

Third: You are part of something bigger than yourself.

When I was younger — and I still have to fight this tendency — but when I was younger, the Christian life could feel kind of small. It was about me and God. Personal relationship. Private faith. And there’s truth in that — God does have a personal relationship with each of us. But if that stays individual, something important gets lost. Because the New Testament almost never talks about faith that way.

Read the New Testament and you find this cosmic, massive, world-redeeming storyline. God is reconciling all things to Himself through Christ (Colossians 1:20). The kingdom of God is advancing. The harvest is plentiful. And somehow — impossibly — God has decided to use you as part of that story.

You’re not a bit player who wandered onto the set. You were placed in the story. Foreknowledge, purpose, calling — God has already sorted all of that. Your job is to show up and represent.

And I think one of the most freeing things you can do is to stop trying to manufacture meaning for your life and start recognizing that God already wrote you into the story He is telling to redeem the world.

You are not an accident. You are not in the wrong job. You are not living in the wrong city by mistake. God has placed you exactly where you are — in your specific context, with your specific relationships, in your specific roles — to be His ambassador in that sphere.

Now, here’s the question this passage eventually forces: What kind of ambassador are you?

Because being an ambassador is an honor — but it’s also a responsibility. And the text here gives us a hint about what that looks like.

In verse 20, Paul says: “We implore you on Christ’s behalf: Be reconciled to God.”

That’s the ambassador’s message. Not “be good enough.” Not “try harder.” Not “get your life together.” It’s one thing: be reconciled to God.

And here’s what’s stunning about that invitation: it doesn’t start with your performance. It starts with God’s initiative. God initiated. Christ accomplished. And now the invitation goes out: come home. Be restored. You’re already welcomed.

That’s the message you carry. Not a list of requirements. Not a call to religion. A call to relationship. Reconciliation. Peace with God.

And that message — that’s what changes everything. Including how you see your own life.

I want to close with this. If you are an ambassador, then your life is not ordinary. Your job is not just a job. Your commute is not just a commute. Your relationships are not accidents. You are representing the King of the universe wherever you go.

That’s not pressure. That’s purpose.

And here’s the thing about ambassadors: they don’t represent their king by accident. They represent him on purpose. With intention. With awareness of what they’re carrying.

That’s what God is inviting you into this week. Not a bigger program. Not a more impressive calling. Just a deeper realization of what you already are — and a willingness to show up in your everyday life as someone who belongs to Him.

You are an ambassador. Act like it.
