Week 2: God Says I Am His Ambassador

Series: Defined - Who God Says You Are | Date: April 19, 2026

Key Passages: 2 Corinthians 5:20, 1 Peter 2:9, Philippians 3:20

INTRODUCTION & BURDEN

You were not just saved from something—you were saved for something. God has given every believer a purpose: to represent Him in the world. As ambassadors of Christ, we carry His message, reflect His character, and invite others into His Kingdom.

This means your life has meaning beyond your job, your routine, or your circumstances. You are part of something eternal. When you understand that you belong to God's Kingdom, your perspective shifts—you begin to live with intentionality.

The burden: To help people understand that they are ambassadors of Christ wherever they are, and that this gives their everyday life eternal significance.

SECTION 1: THEOLOGICAL FOUNDATION

1.1 The Concept of Ambassadorship

An ambassador is a representative who speaks and acts on behalf of a ruler or nation. In the ancient world, ambassadors carried the full authority of the one who sent them.

Biblical usage: The Greek word for ambassador is "presbeutes" (πρεσβευτής), used only in Ephesians 6:20 and 2 Corinthians 5:20.

Paul writes: "We are therefore Christ's ambassadors, as though God were making his appeal through us" (2 Corinthians 5:20).

1.2 The Authority of an Ambassador

  • Authority to represent - We speak for Christ
  • Message from the King - We carry His message
  • The reputation of the Kingdom - We reflect on Him
  • The power to invite - We welcome others into His family

1.3 The Ministry of Reconciliation

The context of 2 Corinthians 5:18-21 is the "ministry of reconciliation." God was reconciling the world to Himself through Christ, and He has committed this message to us.

  • God initiated reconciliation (v. 18)
  • Christ accomplished reconciliation (v. 19)
  • We are ambassadors of reconciliation (v. 20)

1.4 Our Citizenship in Heaven

"But our citizenship is in heaven, and from it we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ." — Philippians 3:20

The implications: Our ultimate loyalty is to heaven. We live as exiles in this world. Our perspective is eternal. We represent a different kingdom.

SECTION 2: BIBLICAL CONTEXT

2.1 2 Corinthians 5:20 - Ambassadors for Christ

Key elements:

  • "We are therefore Christ's ambassadors"
  • "As though God were making his appeal through us"
  • "We implore you on Christ's behalf: Be reconciled to God"

The weight: When we speak, God speaks through us. Our message is from God. Our task is urgent.

2.2 1 Peter 2:9 - A Chosen Race, a Royal Priesthood

Peter draws on Exodus 19:5-6, where God tells Israel they will be "my treasured possession," "a kingdom of priests," and "a holy nation."

Our identity: Chosen race, Royal priesthood, Holy nation, God's own possession

Our purpose: "That you may proclaim the excellencies of Him"

2.3 Philippians 3:20 - Citizenship in Heaven

The contrast: "Our citizenship is in heaven" / We await a Savior from heaven

Living as citizens: We don't belong to this world system. We're here on mission.

SECTION 3: WHAT IT MEANS TO REPRESENT CHRIST

3.1 Everywhere Is Your Mission Field

The myth: Mission is for missionaries overseas. My everyday life is secular.

The reality: Every place is mission field. Your workplace is mission field. Your neighborhood is mission field.

The shift: You don't have to go to mission. You're already there.

3.2 Your Life Carries a Message

The principle: People watch before they listen. Actions speak louder than words.

The tension: We need both word and deed. Without deed, word is incredible. Without word, deed is unclear.

3.3 You Are Part of a Bigger Story

The cosmic context: God is reconciling all things to Himself (Colossians 1:20). The kingdom of God is advancing. You are part of this redemptive work.

SECTION 4: THE PRIVILEGE OF REPRESENTING GOD

4.1 We Speak for God

When we share the gospel, we are not just sharing our opinion—we are speaking for God.

4.2 We Display God's Character

Our lives are a display of God's nature. When people see us, they should see something of God.

4.3 We Invite People Into God's Family

Ambassadors don't just represent—they also invite. Our role is to welcome others into God's family.

SECTION 5: PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS

5.1 Seeing Your Context as Divine Appointment

God has placed you where you are (Acts 17:26-27). Your relationships are not accidents.

5.2 Aligning Lifestyle with Message

The consistency test: Does my work reflect Christ? Does my speech reflect Christ?

5.3 Living with Eternal Perspective

Everything matters for eternity. What seems small is actually significant.

SECTION 6: COMMON OBSTACLES

6.1 Fear of Embarrassment

The solution: Remember your identity. The Spirit gives boldness (Acts 4:31). Love casts out fear (1 John 4:18).

6.2 False Humility

The solution: God uses broken people. Your qualification is Christ in you.

6.3 Complacency

The solution: We are all ambassadors. The harvest is plentiful (Matthew 9:37-38).

SECTION 7: ILLUSTRATIONS

7.1 The Corporate Ambassador

When a CEO represents their company, they carry the company's name, reputation, and message.

7.2 The Royal Representative

In ancient times, when a king sent an ambassador, the ambassador was treated as if the king were present.

7.3 The Foreign Exchange Student

We are representatives of heaven while living on earth.

SECTION 8: SERIES CONNECTIONS

Understanding we are God's children (Week 1) leads naturally to understanding we represent God (Week 2).

  • Week 1: Identity - We are God's children
  • Week 2: Purpose - We represent God
  • Week 3: Action - We live sent
  • Week 4: Outcome - We overcome

SECTION 10: CONCLUSION

You are not just saved from sin—you are saved for mission. God has placed you exactly where you are to represent Him. Your life has meaning beyond your job, your routine, or your circumstances. You are part of something eternal.

The invitation: Stop living for yourself. Start living as an ambassador of the King.
