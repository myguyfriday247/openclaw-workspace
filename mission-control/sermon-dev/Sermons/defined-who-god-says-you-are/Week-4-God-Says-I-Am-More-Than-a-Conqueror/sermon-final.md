**God Says I Am More Than a Conqueror**

Series: Defined — Who God Says You Are  |  Week 4  |  May 3, 2026

ReImage Church — Pastor Chris

---

There's a word that the world uses a lot that actually describes what God wants to produce in you — and most Christians have never heard it unpacked this way.

Most people think identity is *declared* — God says who you are, you believe it, done. But the apostle Paul doesn't just *declare* identity in Romans 8. He describes something being *produced* in you. There's a verb in this passage, not just a noun.

The word is *hyper-nikomen*. "More than conquerors."

That's not a label. That's a transformation.

And the question this week isn't "Do you believe you're a conqueror?" The question is: "Is that identity actually being produced in your life?"

That's what we're going to look at. Let's turn to Romans 8, and I want to read a passage that I think is the climax of everything we've been building toward in this series.

Let me pray for us.

*Father, we come to Your Word today with open hands. We don't want to just hear information — we want to be transformed. Produce something in us. Make us more like Jesus. In His name, amen.*

I want to start with something personal, and I want to be really honest with you.

There have been seasons in my life where I tried to manufacture things in my own strength. Confidence. Peace. Patience. Boldness. And in every one of those seasons, I white-knuckled through it. I exhausted myself. I performed. I pretended. And none of it worked.

And then — in God's kindness — there came a moment where I stopped. I admitted I wasn't enough. I said: "Lord, I can't do this." And something different happened. Grace showed up. The Spirit did what I couldn't do in my own strength.

That moment — the moment you stop performing and start receiving — that's what Romans 8 is about.

See, most of us live like our identity is something we have to *maintain*. Like we're always one bad week away from losing it. You wake up, you try to be a good Christian, you stumble, you feel guilty, you try harder, you fail again. It's exhausting.

But that's not what Paul is describing here. Paul is describing a *Spirit-produced* identity. The Holy Spirit is actively producing five specific identity markers in every believer — not because you're working hard, but because you're remaining in Christ.

Let's look at what they are.

**First: You become the victor.**

Look at verse 28. "We know that in all things God works for good for those who love Him."

Now, I want to slow down here, because I think we've read this verse wrong a lot. Most people read this and think: "All things work together for good — eventually. God will turn my mess into something good — someday. I just have to hold on long enough."

But Paul isn't talking about someday. He's talking about *now*. The word "works" — *sunergei* — it's present tense. Ongoing. God is actively working right now, in this moment, in every circumstance, for your good.

And that changes everything.

Because if God is actively working all things together for your good *right now*, then you don't have to wait for victory. You're not hoping to win. You're not holding out for a better day. You have already inherited the outcome.

The behavior shift is this: instead of spiraling into anxiety when something hard happens — a hard email, a bad diagnosis, a broken relationship — you pause and say: "God is working this for my good." You stop managing the problem and start entrusting it.

I've learned to say that out loud. Not as a formula — as a posture. When something lands and my chest tightens and my mind races, I stop and say: "God, You're working this. Help me trust it."

That's what victor looks like. Not waiting for rescue. Inheriting the outcome.

---

**Second: You become a worshiper.**

Look at verses 31-32. "If God is for us, who can be against us? He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all — how shall He not with Him also freely give us all things?"

Here's the logic: God already gave the most expensive thing He had. His Son. If He did that, He's not going to withhold anything else. Everything else is cheaper. Everything else is easier.

And that means your worship shouldn't be limited to Sunday morning.

When you truly believe that God gave His Son for you, worship stops being a performance and becomes a posture. Your whole life becomes the song.

Romans 12:1 says: "Offer your bodies as living sacrifices, holy and pleasing to God — this is your true and proper worship."

Not "come to church on Sunday." Offer your *body*. How you drive. How you speak to your kids. How you handle the difficult client. How you spend your money. How you respond when someone cuts you off.

Stop asking "Did I go to church?" and start asking "Did I worship?"

That's the shift. And if you're honest, some of you realize you've been doing the first one and missing the second.

---

**Third: You become able.**

Look at verse 34. "Christ is at the right hand of God and also intercedes for us."

Right now. At this very moment. Christ is speaking to the Father on your behalf.

And this is where it gets personal. Because most of us try to live the Christian life in our own strength. We try to be patient. We try to be kind. We try to be generous. We try to control our tongue. And we white-knuckle through all of it and wonder why we're exhausted.

But look at what Jesus is doing for you *right now*. He's interceding. He's speaking up. He's carrying the weight.

And the implication is simple: you don't have to be enough. You just have to stay connected.

When you know Christ is actively interceding for you, weakness stops being a liability. You're not capable in yourself — you're enabled by grace.

The next time you feel overwhelmed, try this before you do anything else: stop and say: "I am not enough for this. I need You." And mean it. And then watch.

That's what able looks like. Not a self-made Christian who has it all together. A Spirit-empowered follower who knows their own insufficiency and rests in Christ's sufficiency.

---

**Fourth: You become courageous.**

Look at verse 37. "In all these things we are MORE THAN CONQUERORS through Him who loved us."

The Greek word here is *hyper-nikomen*. Hyper — more than. Nikomen — conquerors. One who not only wins but gains extraordinary victory.

Here's what I think we miss about this: "more than a conqueror" isn't a feeling you have to generate. It's a disposition that gets produced in you when you know the ending is already secured.

Courage isn't manufactured. It's grown. It's the overflow of knowing something is true that doesn't change.

When you're convinced that nothing can separate you from God's love — death can't, life can't, angels can't, demons can't, the present can't, the future can't, nothing — then courage isn't an act of will. It's the natural result of being secure.

And here's the application: when fear shows up — a hard conversation, a risky decision, a scary diagnosis — don't ask "Do I have enough courage to do this?" Ask a different question: "Am I trusting the One who already won?"

Because the answer is always the same. He already won. And you are on the winning side.

---

**Fifth: You become secure.**

Look at verses 38-39. "Neither death nor life, neither angels nor demons, neither the present nor the future, nor any powers, neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation — nothing will be able to separate us from the love of God."

And then Philippians 3:20: "Our citizenship is in heaven."

Here's the question: where is your security anchored?

Some of you are anchored to your career. If the job is stable, you're okay. Some of you are anchored to your relationships. If they love you, you're loved. Some of you are anchored to your performance. If you're doing enough good things, God is pleased.

And when those things shake — and they will — panic sets in.

But the gospel says something different. It says your foundational identity — your status, your worth, your acceptance — is already settled in heaven. You are a citizen of heaven. Your home address is not here. You are a temporary resident of this world, and your permanent address is with God.

That means nothing that happens in this world can shake you. Not really. Because the thing that matters most — your standing before God — is already secured.

Stop building your sense of worth on shakable things. Anchor to something unshakeable.

---

I want to close with this, becauseRomans 8 has been called the Alps of the New Testament. It's the high peak. Paul's been building to this for eight chapters, and at the end he can't contain himself.

He starts with "no condemnation." He moves through "no separation." He lands on this enormous, triumphant declaration: nothing — absolutely nothing — can separate you from the love of God.

Now, I want to bring this home in a way that I think is deeply relevant to where a lot of us are right now.

Abraham Maslow spent his career studying what humans need to feel whole. He built a pyramid — and he got close. Security. Belonging. Self-actualization. He was right about the needs.

But here's the gap in his pyramid: psychology can *describe* the need for unconditional, unshakeable acceptance — but it can't *supply* it.

That's the gap only the gospel fills.

The top tier of Maslow's pyramid — the thing at the very peak that every human is reaching for — is a love that doesn't depend on what you do. An acceptance that isn't earned. A worth that isn't based on your performance.

And you don't have to climb for it. You just have to receive it.

You don't have to perform your way into God's favor. You don't have to earn your adoption. You don't have to prove you're worthy. You just have to receive what God is offering: love, acceptance, belonging — all of it given, not earned.

If you've been working to earn God's love — stop. Right now. Receive it. It's yours. Not because you performed well this week. Not because you showed up enough. Because He gave it to you before you were born.

This is who you are: a citizen of heaven. Secure. Loved. Accepted.

Stop performing. Start receiving.
