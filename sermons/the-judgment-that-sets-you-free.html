Point 1: Your Sin Cannot Be Hidden from God's Justice

I'm going to have four points today, four things I want us to see about God's justice.

Number one: Your sin cannot be hidden from God's justice.

I know a lot of times we tend to fall on the message of the gospel on the side of mercy, and that is a big part of it, and we're going to talk about that. But God is a just God.

Because God is a holy God, because he's holy and he's perfect and he's just, he is a person of justice. And that means that there's this unfortunate truth: sin must be punished. It must be punished.

When we're looking at other people's sin, we want justice. But then when we realize that we also commit sins, we want to cling to mercy. And we want to forget that God is a God of justice.

In this verse: "I am against you, says the Lord of hosts. I will lift your skirts over your face. I will show the nations your nakedness and the kingdoms your shame."

Sometimes we try and hide things in plain sight. And the Assyrians did that too. Instead of basically trying to say, oh, we're not bad people, they said, oh, no, we're bad, but look at how good our badness is. They tried to make it seem like it was a good thing. How often do we do that? How often do we say, oh, no, no, no, this really isn't a bad thing? Well, whose standard are we using to determine whether it's good or whether it's bad? We're using ours. We're using God's.

The Conclusion: The Cross — Where Justice Meets Mercy

And that's where the story turns, because God's justice is actually good news.

God's judgment does a couple of things:

Number one: Whenever God brings judgment, it frees the people who have been oppressed. That's what we see in this story. God's judgment comes in and it frees the children of Israel who've been oppressed.

The beauty of the story of the entire Bible is that God's judgment doesn't have to be borne by you.

What do you mean, it doesn't have to be borne by me? That is the mystery of the Bible.

Because we could look at this story and we could say, OK, this is a story about a historical thing that happened, but we know everything in the Old Testament points us to Jesus.

And what is the big evil, the big thing that can't be overcome, the thing that dominates every person in humanity and the thing that keeps them all enslaved and oppressed? It's sin.

And Jesus came. The message of the cross is this place we've talked about where justice and mercy meet because God is just, He could not NOT punish us because punishment had to be doled out. And that's why it became necessary for Jesus to take our sin upon Himself so that God could give Him the punishment we deserve.

The idea that Jesus would take sin upon Himself when He had no sin, meaning He did not deserve to die. The Bible tells us quite clearly: the wages of sin is death.

So you and I, we are those people we point out and we say they need to get judgment. Jesus was not that person, which means because He did not deserve the punishment, He could take the punishment.

It wouldn't make sense if two inmates are on death row and one of them comes up and says, hey, you can kill me for that person's crime. No, we're already killing you for you. We can't. It doesn't work that way. But Jesus could.

And so in Jesus, God's justice can be fulfilled.

What I hope we see from the book of Nahum is that God is a God of justice. And He serves out justice and it is gruesome. An entire civilization was wiped out because God is a God of justice. God also killed His Son because God is a God of justice. But He did it so that you could be freed from the oppression.

As I was reading this at the end, there's this verse that just kind of stood out to me: "All who hear of your destruction will clap their hands for joy."

Joy. And I just thought, you know, if the people of the world looked at the fall of Nineveh and rejoiced because such an evil had been eliminated, how much more should we rejoice?

And I'm reminded of the scripture that says it sounds almost jubilant: Oh, death, where is your sting? Because our oppressor — sin and death — that price has been paid, justice has been served.

And so then when we look back to this and we say, oh, look, God's justice is final. Oh, yes, it's final. And it's done with it because we can run to Jesus.

Which means when we mess up, we don't have to try and skirt justice. We don't have to try and avoid it. We can go and experience it because the great exchange that takes place is that Jesus exchanges His life for ours.

He takes our punishment, He gives us His life. He takes our place, He gives us His righteousness.

And the message of the gospel that sometimes we even after we give our lives to Christ seem to forget is that the price has been paid because God's judgment is final. And God has made His judgment that Jesus was the acceptable sacrifice for us.

And so there's no reason for you to try and hide anything. You don't have to pretend to be perfect, not with God. You shouldn't have to do it with the rest of us.

You know why? Because as much as you're trying to hide that thing that you don't want us to know about, we got three things we don't want you to know about either.

We're all just people who are only here because God's justice is final, but His mercy is that we didn't have to be the ones to pay the price for that judgment.
