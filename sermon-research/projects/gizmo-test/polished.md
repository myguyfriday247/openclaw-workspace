# Gizmo Test Brief

## 1. Research Assignment Summary

This brief examines Judges 6 and the revelation of God as *Jehovah Shalom*—the LORD is Peace—exploring how divine peace is extended to a fearful leader before circumstances change.

## 2. Passage Overview

When we meet Gideon, he is hiding—threshing wheat in a winepress to keep it from the Midianites. He is not a man at peace. Yet God addresses him as a "mighty warrior" and commissions him to deliver Israel. Gideon's response is saturated with fear: fear of inadequacy, fear of his clan's insignificance, fear even of having seen the angel of the LORD face to face. And it is precisely into that fear that God speaks *shalom*. Before a single sword is drawn, before Midian's oppression lifts, Gideon builds an altar and names it *Jehovah Shalom*. The peace comes first—not as a reward for victory, but as the ground on which obedience becomes possible.

## 3. Main Theological Burden of the Text

The core theological claim of this passage is that God's peace is rooted in His presence, not in changed circumstances. Gideon does not receive peace because the battle is won; he receives peace because the LORD has met him. This distinction matters pastorally: *shalom* in the Old Testament is never merely the absence of conflict but the fullness of well-being that flows from proximity to God Himself. The altar Gideon raises stands as a testimony—not that everything is now fine, but that God is here, and that is enough to move forward.
