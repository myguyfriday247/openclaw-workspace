# Final Sermon Brief Template

Produce the final sermon research brief in markdown.

## Requirements

- 20–30 page depth
- pastoral but academically informed tone
- footnote-style superscripts
- bibliography
- distinct classic and modern commentary sections
- primary and secondary cross references
- research-based implications for life and behavior
- no sermon outline
- no manuscript writing

## Required Sections

### 1. Research Assignment Summary
A clean 2-3 paragraph summary of what this research is trying to accomplish.

### 2. Passage Overview
Key background, structure, and movement of the text.

### 3. Main Theological Burden
The central theological claim the sermon will carry.

### 4. Key Themes & Observations
Deep exploration of the main themes, with supporting evidence from the text.

### 5. Cross-References
- Primary cross-references (same book/closely related)
- Secondary cross-references (broader Scriptural witness)

### 6. Theological Implications
How this passage shapes doctrine and信徒 understanding.

### 7. Pastoral Application
Research-based implications for life and behavior in a local church context.

### 8. Scholarly Sources & References
**For each source, include:**
- Type tag: `[Commentary]`, `[Theology]`, `[Current Voice]`, `[Historical]`
- Author, Title, Publication info
- Page numbers for key quotes or arguments
- One-line note on usefulness

Example:
```
[Commentary] John Calvin, *Commentary on Romans* (1551), pp. 84-87. 
  Useful for: understanding Calvin's view on the hardening of hearts.

[Current Voice] Tim Keller, "Romans 9 on Election" (Gospel in Life sermon series). 
  Useful for: framing election as invitation rather than rejection.
```

### 9. Go Deeper — Pastor Resources
Books, commentaries, and resources aligned with the passage and topic:

- **For Study:** Commentary or theological work suited for deep preparation
- **For Illustration:** Book or resource with vivid, usable illustrations
- **For Preaching:** Sermon or message from a trusted voice on this passage

Tag each: `[For Study]`, `[For Illustration]`, `[For Preaching]`

Example:
```
[For Study] James M. Boice, *Romans* (Baker Commentary), Vol. 2, pp. 541-558.
  Deals directly with the pastoral problem of divine sovereignty and human responsibility.

[For Preaching] John Piper, "The Justice of God" (Desiring God sermon).
  Excellent framing of God's righteousness as display, not obstacle.
```

### 10. Research Gap Note
If there are areas where the research is thin, note them honestly so the pastor knows where additional work may be needed.
